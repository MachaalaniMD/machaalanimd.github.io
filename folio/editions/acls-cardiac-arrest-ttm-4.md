# ACLS: Cardiac Arrest & TTM
> Push hard and fast, shock VT/VF early, give epinephrine every 3–5 minutes, and hunt the H's and T's. After ROSC: MAP >65, SpO2 92–98%, a 12-lead ECG, and temperature control for anyone not following commands. {AHA 2020; AHA 2023}

## During the code
:::key CPR quality
- Compress 2–2.4 inches deep at 100–120/min; allow full recoil; minimize interruptions
- **No airway:** 30:2 compressions to bag-mask breaths. **With ETT:** a breath every 6–8 s; switch compressors every 2 min
- Rhythm check as soon as pads are on: **shockable → defibrillate; non-shockable → epinephrine**
- Pulse/rhythm check every 2 minutes
:::
| Rhythm | Do |
|---|---|
| **VT / VF** | Shock (biphasic 120–200 J; monophasic 360 J; unknown → maximum). Repeat at same or higher energy. Epinephrine 1 mg q3–5 min. With the 2nd/3rd epinephrine dose: amiodarone or lidocaine. No ROSC after 3 shocks → AP pad placement and double sequential defibrillation |
| **PEA / asystole** | Epinephrine 1 mg q3–5 min; continue CPR; consider an advanced airway |

### Code drug doses
| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Epinephrine** | 1 mg IV/IO q3–5 min |
| **Amiodarone** | 300 mg first dose, 150 mg second |
| **Lidocaine** | 1–1.5 mg/kg first, 0.5–0.75 mg/kg second |
| **Magnesium sulfate** | 2 g IV for torsades |
| **Hyperkalemia** | CaCl2 1 g; bicarbonate 1–2 amps; D50 1–2 amps + insulin 10 U IV |
| **Naloxone** | ≥2 mg IV if pulseless; 0.2–1 mg IV if apneic with a pulse |

### Reversible causes: H's and T's
| H's | T's |
|---|---|
| Hypovolemia; hemorrhage | Thrombosis: coronary (ACS) or pulmonary (PE) |
| Hypoxia | Tension pneumothorax |
| H⁺ (acidosis) | Tamponade |
| Hypo/hyperkalemia | Toxins (drugs, accidents) |
| Hypothermia | |

### Code tasks (ABCDEF)
**A**ccess (IV >> IO), **A**irway · **B**ackboard · **C**ompression quality, **C**ode status (always confirm) · **D**efibrillator, **D**rips · **E**CMO / **E**mbolus (if ECMO is possible, page within 10 min; consider tenecteplase) · **F**amily (call the HCP). Monitoring: telemetry, pads, continuous O2, BP cuff.

### Logistics
- **Access:** IV first; IO if needed (drug delivery may be worse with tibial sites). {AHA 2020}
- **STAT labs:** ABG, CBC, BMP, LFTs, lactate, type and screen, coags, fibrinogen, troponin
- **Capnography:** keep ETCO2 >10, ideally >20. **ETCO2 <10 after 20 min of CPR** in an intubated patient is ~90% sensitive for no ROSC.
- **Pregnancy:** manual left uterine displacement; IVs above the diaphragm; stop IV Mg and give CaCl; **resuscitative hysterotomy if no ROSC within 5 min**; consider TTM after ROSC. Maternal code mnemonic: ABCDEFGH.

## PE during a code
:::warn Thrombolysis for known or suspected PE
**Tenecteplase** (preferred over alteplase), IV push over 5 seconds, by weight:
| Weight | Dose |
|---|---|
| <60 kg | 30 mg |
| 60–69 kg | 35 mg |
| 70–79 kg | 40 mg |
| 80–89 kg | 45 mg |
| ≥90 kg | 50 mg |
- **No tenecteplase:** alteplase 50 mg IV over 2 min; repeat once at 15 min if no ROSC
- **Continue CPR ≥15 min** after lysis
- **Heparin drip** once the PTT falls below 2× normal
- **Absolute contraindications:** prior ICH; ischemic stroke or head trauma within 3 months; intracranial tumor or AVM; suspected aortic dissection; active bleeding
:::

## ECMO for cardiac arrest
- Page the ECMO team early, ideally within 10 min of the code starting.
- **Indications:** age <75, no ROSC within 5 min, ETCO2 >10.
- **Contraindications:** BMI >45, ETCO2 <10, lactate >18, pH <7.0, PaO2 <50 on ABG, severe AR, aortic dissection, uncontrollable bleeding. Discuss major comorbidities with the ECMO team.
- **Favorable:** initial shockable rhythm, short low-flow time. {Lancet 2020;396:1807; JAMA 2022;327:737; NEJM 2023;388:299}

## After ROSC
| Step | Targets and actions |
|---|---|
| **Stabilize** | MAP >65 {NEJM 2022;387:1456}, norepinephrine first; SpO2 92–98% {NEJM 2022;387:1467}; PaCO2 35–45 {NEJM 2023;389:45} |
| **Find the cause** | 12-lead ECG; other H's and T's. STEMI on the post-ROSC ECG → activate the cath lab. Possible ischemic cause (ischemic ECG, recent PCI/CABG, VT/VF arrest) → cardiology. Hypothermia doesn't preclude PCI {Resuscitation 2010;81:398} |
| **Temperature control** | Not following commands → TTM (below). Consult neuro ICU. Consider head CT, central line, arterial line |
| **Prognosis** | Multimodal, ≥72 h off all sedation: serial neuro exams, continuous EEG ASAP, MRI brain >48–72 h, SSEPs >48 h, NSE at 24, 48 and 72 h {Neurocrit Care 2023}. See *Neuroprognostication* |

## Targeted temperature management
- **Why:** fever worsens neurologic injury after ischemia–reperfusion. {Arch Intern Med 2001} TTM is Class I for anyone not following commands after arrest. {AHA 2023} The goal is to prevent fever and to find and treat infection aggressively.
- **Fever sources:** ischemia–reperfusion inflammation, drug fever, infection. Infection risk is high (aspiration, gut ischemia with translocation): pan-culture and consider empiric antibiotics. On a cooling device, fever shows up as a change in the device's cooling pattern.
- **Evidence:** hypothermia (33 °C) didn't improve 6-month survival or neurologic outcome vs normothermia (<37.5 °C). {NEJM 2021;384:2283} Subgroups (e.g. non-shockable rhythms) are uncertain {NEJM 2019;381:2327}; no specific temperature is recommended for any group (Class 2b). {AHA 2023}

| Strategy | How |
|---|---|
| **Normothermia (≤37.5 °C)** | Allow passive rewarming to 37 °C, then a temperature-control device to keep T <37.5 °C for 72 h |
| **Mild hypothermia (33–36 °C)** | Selected case by case with neurology. Device ASAP; reach target within 4 h of ROSC; hold 24 h, then normothermia for 48 h (72 h total). Rewarm 0.25 °C/h. Watch glucose and electrolytes. Relative contraindications: refractory hypotension, recent head trauma, active bleeding, major surgery <14 days |

- **Sedation:** continuous sedation and analgesia during mild hypothermia (propofol preferred; hydromorphone or fentanyl).
- **Shivering, stepwise** {Neurocrit Care 2011;14:389}:
  1. Acetaminophen 650 mg q6h (PR/PO, then IV); Mg >3
  2. Skin counter-warming + dexmedetomidine, propofol or hydromorphone boluses
  3. Propofol + hydromorphone drips; consider buspirone 30 mg q8h
  4. Neuromuscular blockade
