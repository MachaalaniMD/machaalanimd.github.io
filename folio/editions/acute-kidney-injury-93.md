# Acute Kidney Injury
> A Cr rise of ≥0.3 in 48 h, or 1.5× baseline in 7 days, or low urine output. Sort it into pre-renal, intrinsic or post-renal with history, UA, urine chemistries and a bladder scan; fix volume, stop nephrotoxins, dose meds for the real GFR. {KDIGO 2012}

## Define and stage
| Stage | Creatinine | Urine output |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Rise ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 h, or 1.5–1.9× baseline within 7 days | <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 h |
| **2** | 2–2.9× baseline | <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥12 h |
| **3** | ≥3× baseline, Cr ≥4, eGFR <35 (age <18), or dialysis | <0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥24 h, or anuria ≥12 h |

:::tip Reading creatinine
- Cr estimates GFR only at steady state. If Cr rises >1 mg/dL per day, assume GFR <10.
- Some drugs raise Cr without lowering GFR (BUN stays flat): trimethoprim, cimetidine, dronedarone.
- **BUN up more than Cr:** pre- or post-renal, upper GI bleed, steroids. **Cr up more than BUN:** rhabdo, AIN, TMP-SMX, vancomycin, poor nutrition.
:::

## Work it up
1. **History and exam:** BP (high or low), volume status, contrast and med exposures, recent infection (IgA nephropathy 1–2 days later; post-strep GN 10–14 days later), active infection (sepsis can cause ATN without hypotension {JASN 2011;22:999}), trauma or being found down (rhabdo), rash (AIN, vasculitis)
2. **Urinalysis** (see *Urinalysis*): unexplained blood + protein → think GN and call renal
3. **Urine chemistries** (below)
4. **Urine sediment:** casts, RBC morphology
5. **Bladder scan, then renal ultrasound** for hydronephrosis. Without a suggestive history, <1% of ultrasounds for AKI find obstruction, but they can show chronic disease. {BMC Nephrol 2013;14:188}
6. **Trend** Cr, urine output, BP and electrolytes to see if empiric treatment works
7. **Suspect glomerular disease?** Send serologies (see *Glomerular Disease*); biopsy if it would change treatment

| Test | Suggests | Caveats |
|---|---|---|
| **FENa** | <1% pre-renal; >2% ATN | Only validated in oliguric AKI; useless on diuretics or after fluids; unreliable in CKD, heart failure, cirrhosis. GN, rhabdo and contrast can also give <1%. Mostly useful to argue against HRS (high FENa) {J Hosp Med 2016;11:77} |
| **FEUrea** | <35% pre-renal | Use when on diuretics {Kid Int 2002;62:2223} |
| **Urine osm** | >500 pre-renal (high ADH) | |
| **Urine albumin/protein ratio** | >0.4 glomerular rather than tubulointerstitial (Sn 88%, Sp 99%) | Send serum albumin, urine total protein, microalbumin and Cr if UA shows protein {Nephrol Dial Transplant 2012;27:1534} |
| **Timed urine Cr** | 12 or 24 h collection estimates CrCl when Cr is changing | |
| **Urine eosinophils** | Not recommended | Poor test for AIN: >1% has Sn 31%, Sp 68% {J Hosp Med 2017;12:343} |

## Causes
| Pre-renal | Intrinsic | Post-renal |
|---|---|---|
| **Low volume:** bleeding, GI or skin losses, diuretics, osmotic diuresis, cerebral salt wasting | **ATN:** ischemia, sepsis, nephrotoxins (see StatPearls list), contrast, rhabdo | **Retention:** BPH, meds, neurogenic bladder, blocked Foley |
| **Low effective volume:** heart failure (cardiorenal), cirrhosis (hepatorenal), nephrotic syndrome, sepsis/third spacing | **AIN:** most often NSAIDs, β-lactams, PPIs | **Bilateral obstruction** (or a single kidney/transplant): stones, malignancy, retroperitoneal fibrosis (radiation, IgG4 disease), congenital anomalies |
| **Altered renal hemodynamics:** NSAIDs/COX-2, ACEi/ARBs, abdominal compartment syndrome; relative hypotension | **Glomerular** (GN, RPGN), **vascular** (TMA, atheroemboli), **crystals**, **myeloma cast nephropathy** | |

## Treat
1. **Volume and nephrotoxins:** fluids and hold diuretics if dry; diurese if overloaded. Stop NSAIDs, ACEi/ARBs; avoid hyperglycemia and contrast. Empiric diuretics don't help oliguria. {JAMA 2002;288:2547} For large-volume resuscitation or critical illness, prefer LR over NS. {NEJM 2018;378:819; NEJM 2018;378:829}
2. **Dose meds for the real GFR:** eGFR assumes a steady state. If Cr is rising, clearance is lower than the eGFR; if anuric, assume GFR 0. Use Lexicomp and review the MAR with pharmacy.
3. **Complications:**
   - **Hyperkalemia:** ECG and telemetry; calcium, insulin/dextrose; then remove K (Lokelma, bowel regimen, furosemide); low-K diet (see *Potassium Disorders*)
   - **Hyperphosphatemia:** binder (sevelamer when phos >5.5, or calcium acetate depending on Ca); avoid IV calcium if Ca × phos >70; low-phos diet
   - **Metabolic acidosis:** oral or IV bicarbonate (see *Acid–Base Disorders*)
   - **Uremic bleeding:** DDAVP 0.3 mcg/kg IV (onset 1 h, lasts 4–8 h)

:::warn Dialysis indications: AEIOU
**A**cidosis (especially pH <7.0, refractory to bicarbonate) · **E**lectrolytes (refractory hyperkalemia) · **I**ntoxication (lithium, ethylene glycol, metformin, salicylates, theophylline) · **O**verload refractory to diuretics · **U**remia (encephalopathy, neuropathy, pericarditis)
:::

## When to call renal overnight
- **Acidosis:** pH <7.1 (or <7.2 with severe AKI) without a quickly reversible cause like DKA. Bridge with bicarbonate pushes and an isotonic bicarbonate drip; intubate and hyperventilate if the patient can't compensate.
- **Hyperkalemia:** ECG changes or arrhythmia, or K >6.5 (>5.5 if rising fast, e.g. rhabdo or GI bleed). Intermittent HD clears K faster than CVVH if the patient can tolerate it.
- **Hyponatremia:** severe (<120), symptomatic (low GCS, seizures), or needing 3% saline ± a DDAVP clamp
- **Anuria with decompensated heart failure:** Foley; watch urine output, mixed venous O2 and signs of shock (LFTs, Cr, lactate, cardiac index). Raising cardiac output (diuresis or HD, ± inotropes) raises GFR.
- **Ingestions:** lithium, ethylene glycol, methanol (high osmolar gap, then high anion gap), metformin with organ damage
- **Suspected RPGN:** urgent consult for pulse steroids ± plasmapheresis
- **Scleroderma renal crisis:** sudden severe hypertension then oliguric failure in diffuse scleroderma. Captopril, start 6.25–12.5 mg and titrate q4h to the maximum tolerated dose. Avoid steroids.

## Specific causes
::: AIN | Drug-induced interstitial nephritis
Stop the drug. Consider prednisone 40–60 mg daily for 1–2 weeks if biopsy-proven or highly likely (weak evidence). {CJASN 2018;13:1851}
:::
::: Cardiorenal (type 1) | Heart failure causing AKI
- Mostly congestion (high venous pressure lowering trans-renal perfusion pressure), more than low forward flow. {Nat Rev Neph 2013;9:99; Circ 2019;139:e840}
- **Decongest:** loop diuretic ± thiazide (metolazone, chlorothiazide); see *Advanced Diuresis*. Trend Cr against net balance and weight; expect a lag.
- Low-dose dopamine and nesiritide don't help. {JAMA 2013;310:2533} Ultrafiltration removes fluid no better and worsens kidney function. {NEJM 2012;367:2296}
:::
::: Contrast-associated AKI | Cr up ≥0.5 or 25% within 48–72 h of contrast
- Starts at 24–48 h, peaks at 3–5 days, resolves by ~10 days; usually non-oliguric. True risk is probably lower than once thought. {Ann Emerg Med 2017;69:577; Nat Rev Nephrol 2017;13:169}
- **Risk:** large or hyperosmolar contrast load, intra-arterial injection (cath), diabetes, proteinuria, existing AKI
- **Prevention** if GFR <30, or 30–45 with diabetes: consider IV NS. If treating overload, just hold diuretics on the contrast day. Bicarbonate and NAC add nothing {NEJM 2018;378:603}; neither does timing dialysis around contrast in anuric patients. {Am J Med 2012;125:66}
- Weigh this against the harm of delaying a diagnosis.
:::
::: Crystal nephropathy | Drug or metabolite crystals
Stop the drug. Ethylene glycol: fomepizole ± HD. Tumor lysis: rasburicase ± HD. Acyclovir: fluids and diuresis to urine output >150 mL/h.
:::
::: Myeloma cast nephropathy | Light chains
- **Diagnose:** urine protein/Cr, SPEP, UPEP, serum free light chains; biopsy if unclear
- **Treat:** IV fluids to urine output >3 L/day (diurese if overloaded) and chemotherapy with oncology. Plasmapheresis benefit is unclear (light chains rebound).
:::
::: Rhabdomyolysis | AKI unlikely unless CK >20,000
- Aggressive NS to urine output >250 mL/h. Isotonic bicarbonate if marked acidosis and normal calcium, though not proven better than NS. {NEJM 2009;361:62}
- Watch for hyperkalemia and hypocalcemia. Continue fluids until CK <5000, diuresing as needed once resuscitated.
:::
::: Post-renal | Obstruction
Foley; α-blocker; 5α-reductase inhibitor (slow to work); urology or IR for nephrostomy if needed.
:::
::: Hepatorenal | See Hepatorenal Syndrome
See *Hepatorenal Syndrome*.
:::
