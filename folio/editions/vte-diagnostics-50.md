# VTE Diagnostics
> Estimate pretest probability (Wells, Geneva), use D-dimer only when probability is low, image when it isn't, then risk-stratify PE by hemodynamics, RV strain and troponin. {ASH: Blood Adv 2018;2:3226; ESC: EHJ 2019;41:543; JAMA 2018;320:1583}

## Diagnose
:::key Pathway
1. **Pretest probability:** Wells (leg DVT and PE), Constans (arm DVT {Thromb Haemost 2008;99:202}), Geneva (PE)
2. **Low** (or moderate for DVT) → D-dimer, or PERC for PE. Negative → excluded. Positive → image.
3. **High** (or moderate for PE) → image directly
:::
- Wells is validated for outpatients; it can't rule out DVT in hospitalized patients. {JAMA 2015;175:1112}
- **DVT:** venous Doppler of the leg (LENI) or arm (UENI).

### D-dimer (normal <500)
- **Normal + low pretest probability excludes VTE.** {NEJM 2003;349:1227; JAMA 2006;295:199; Thromb Haemost 2009;101:886}
- **Adjusted cutoffs** reduce imaging without missing more PE:
  - **Age-adjusted:** age ×10 if over 50 {JAMA 2014;311:1117}
  - **Probability-adjusted:** <1000 with low clinical probability {NEJM 2019;381:2125}
- **Also raised by:** arterial thrombosis (MI, stroke, AF/intracardiac thrombus, limb ischemia), DIC, cancer, inflammation/infection, ESLD, heart failure, CKD, aortic dissection, trauma, surgery

### Imaging for PE
| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| **CT pulmonary angiogram** | Test of choice; may find an alternative diagnosis {NEJM 2006;354:2317} |
| **V/Q scan** | Validated {JAMA 1990;263:2753}. When CT is contraindicated; needs a normal CXR; not available urgently; often indeterminate |
| **Leg Dopplers** | If CT and V/Q aren't possible: a positive study justifies treatment; a negative one doesn't exclude PE |
| **Echo** | Risk stratification, not diagnosis; clot in transit or new RV strain gives a presumptive diagnosis when time-critical |
| **ABG** | Hypoxemia with high A–a gradient (normal in ~20%), respiratory alkalosis |

## Risk-stratify PE
| Risk | Unstable (shock, SBP <90, arrest) | Troponin / BNP up | RV dysfunction on imaging | PESI III–V or sPESI ≥1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Yes | (+) | (+) | (+) |
| **Intermediate–high** | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Intermediate–low** | No | One of the two | One of the two | Yes |
| **Low** | No | No | No | No |

- **Instability + confirmed PE or RV dysfunction = high risk** on its own; scores and biomarkers aren't needed. Syncope and clot in transit also count as high risk.
- **Biomarkers:** hsTnT ≥14 (age <75) or ≥45 (age >75) {ERJ 2015;45:1323}; high NT-proBNP {ERJ 2014;43:1669}
- **RV dysfunction:** CT RV/LV ratio >0.9 {JCM 2024;13:257}; echo: dilated RV, flat septum, moderate–severe TR, McConnell sign (free-wall akinesis sparing the apex {Circ 2008;118:517}), low TAPSE {JEM 2020;58:449}
- **Scores:** PESI and simplified PESI predict 30-day morbidity and mortality; Bova predicts complications in stable patients.

## Recognize it
### DVT
- **Symptoms and signs:** pain, warmth, redness or cyanosis, asymmetric swelling, palpable cord, superficial venous distention, Homan sign; can be silent {Arch IM 1998;158:2315}
- **Proximal:** iliac, femoral, popliteal. **Distal:** calf veins (anterior/posterior tibial, peroneal, soleal, gastrocnemius).
- **Massive iliofemoral DVT:** phlegmasia alba dolens (swelling, pain) → **phlegmasia cerulea dolens** (cyanosis, venous gangrene) needs immediate thrombolysis.
- **May-Thurner:** right iliac artery compresses the left common iliac vein → left leg swelling, venous claudication, severe insufficiency, with or without DVT.
- **Arm DVT:** usually catheter-related (PICC > central line > port). {Hematology ASH 2014;1:306}
- **Differential:** superficial thrombophlebitis, cellulitis, arterial occlusion, varicose veins, lymphedema, ruptured Baker cyst, chronic venous insufficiency

### PE
- **Symptoms:** dyspnea, pleuritic pain, cough, orthopnea, leg pain/swelling, syncope, hemoptysis, sweating, palpitations; can be silent {Chest 1991;100(3):598}
- **Signs:** tachypnea, tachycardia, hypoxemia, hypotension, crackles, S4, loud P2, JVD, fever, wheeze, RV heave, rub, S3
- **ECG:** sinus tachycardia, AF/flutter, RAD, RBBB, inferior Q waves, anterior ST/T changes, S1Q3T3 {ERJ 2005;25:843}

### Risk factors (Virchow: stasis, injury, hypercoagulability) {Circulation 2003;107:I9}
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| HF or AF admission, or MI, within 3 months; hip/knee replacement; leg fracture; major surgery or trauma; spinal cord injury | Bed rest >3 days; prolonged sitting (travel); older age; laparoscopic surgery; obesity {Circ 2008;117:93}; varicose veins; pregnancy; CKD {J Thromb Haemost 2014;12:1449}; diabetes; cirrhosis {Thromb Haemost 2017;117:139} |
