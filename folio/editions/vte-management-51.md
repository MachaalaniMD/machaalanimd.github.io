# VTE Management
> Anticoagulate every proximal DVT and PE for at least 3 months, with a DOAC unless there's a reason not to. Match PE treatment to risk: thrombolysis for high-risk, LMWH and close watching for intermediate, outpatient care for low. {CHEST: Chest 2021;160:545; ESC: EHJ 2020;41:543; ASH: Blood Adv 2020;19:4693}

## PE by risk
::: High risk | Shock, SBP <90, arrest
- **Fluids cautiously:** a small trial if CVP is low; too much distends the RV, ischemia and septal bowing lower output (RV death spiral)
- **Support:** dobutamine for low output; norepinephrine, epinephrine or vasopressin for BP
- **Oxygen:** high-flow nasal cannula preferred. Intubation is high-risk (induction and positive pressure lower venous return and can collapse the RV).
- **Arrest:** with a pulse → alteplase; pulseless → weight-based tenecteplase and 15 min of CPR; consider VA-ECMO (see *ACLS: Cardiac Arrest*)
- **Anticoagulate:** LMWH or UFH with bolus
- **Thrombolysis:** systemic (catheter-directed in some cases)
- **Embolectomy** (catheter or surgical) if lysis is contraindicated or fails; surgery for clot in transit (RA/RV) or across a PFO
:::
::: Intermediate risk | RV strain and/or troponin, stable
- **LMWH preferred** over UFH (reaches range faster), unless collapse is likely, lysis is planned, CrCl <30 or severe obesity
- **Switch to an oral agent** after 48–72 h of stability (intermediate–high) or 24 h (intermediate–low)
- **Lysis for select patients** (catheter-directed preferred): developing shock, respiratory failure, moderate–severe RV dysfunction (RV hypokinesis, RVSP >40) with very high troponin/BNP. {Circ 2011;123:1788}
- Routine lysis here prevents decompensation but doesn't clearly lower mortality, and adds major bleeding and hemorrhagic stroke. {NEJM 2014;370:1402; JACC 2017;69:1536; Am J Med 2019;132:240}
:::
::: Low risk | No strain, stable
- Anticoagulate (below); discharge if nothing else needs admission (Hestia score)
- **Isolated subsegmental PE without DVT:** rule out proximal DVT. Low recurrence risk → consider surveillance imaging; otherwise anticoagulate. {Chest 2021;160:e545}
:::

### Thrombolysis {Circ 2014;129:479; JAMA 2014;311:2414; Am J Card 2019;123:684; CDSR 2021;4:CD00437}
May lower mortality and improve hemodynamics vs heparin alone, at the cost of more bleeding and ICH.
- **Systemic:** with a pulse, alteplase 50–100 mg over 2 h. In arrest, weight-based tenecteplase over 5 s. Hold heparin during the infusion (don't delay lysis if LMWH was already given); restart when PTT <2× ULN. Fibrinogen q6h.
- **Absolute contraindications:** CNS tumor, CNS surgery or trauma <2–3 months, prior ICH, active bleeding, non-hemorrhagic stroke <3 months
- **Catheter-directed** (into the pulmonary artery, much less drug): high bleeding risk, failed systemic lysis, or intermediate-risk PE. Ultrasound-assisted (EKOS) may help break up clot. {JACC Card Interv 2015;8:1382; Am J Card 2019;124:1470}

## DVT
| | Proximal (popliteal, femoral, iliac) | Distal (calf veins) |
|---|---|---|
| **Treat?** | **Anticoagulate** unless contraindicated, with or without symptoms | Serial ultrasound **or** anticoagulate |
| **Serial imaging if** | | No symptoms, low extension risk, or high bleeding risk. Repeat at 1–2 weeks (⅓ extend; less often in muscular veins) |
| **Anticoagulate if** | | Severe symptoms; extension risk (positive D-dimer, >5 cm, multiple veins, >7 mm wide, near proximal veins, unprovoked, cancer, prior VTE, inpatient); extends toward proximal veins on repeat imaging; patient preference |

- **Agent:** DOAC > warfarin > LMWH; **with cancer:** DOAC > LMWH > warfarin. Dosing: *Anticoagulation Agents*, *Anticoagulation Management*.
- **Arm DVT** (brachial, axillary, subclavian): fewer complications, same treatment. Catheter-related: keep the line if it's needed, working and not infected; anticoagulate catheter-related or proximal clots. Lysis only for select patients (severe symptoms). {NEJM 2011;364:861; JCM 2020;9:2069}
- **Superficial vein thrombosis:** reduced-dose DOAC for 45 days in some patients {Chest 2021;160:545}; otherwise heat and elevation.
- **Post-thrombotic syndrome:** long-term venous insufficiency; compression ± endovascular or surgical treatment. {Cardiovasc Diagn Ther 2016;6:623}

## How long
- **At least 3 months for everyone.**
- **6–12 months:** persistent but reversible risk factor
- **Indefinite:** unprovoked with low bleeding risk, active cancer, or a persistent risk factor (IBD, nephrotic syndrome, etc.)
- **If anticoagulation stops,** offer reduced-dose DOAC for extended prevention; aspirin is inferior but better than nothing. {NEJM 2017;376:1211; CHEST 2021;160:2247}

### Bleeding risk on anticoagulation
| Risk factors | Bleeding (first 3 months) | After 3 months |
|---|---|---|
| **0 (low)** | 1.6% | 0.8%/year |
| **1 (moderate)** | 3.2% | 1.6%/year |
| **≥2 (high)** | 12.8% | ≥6.5%/year |

**Risk factors:** age >65–75, prior bleeding, cancer, kidney or liver failure, thrombocytopenia, prior stroke, diabetes, anemia, antiplatelet drugs, poor anticoagulation control, poor functional status, recent surgery, frequent falls, alcohol use disorder, NSAIDs.

### IVC filter
- **Only** if anticoagulation is contraindicated (active bleeding, recent or upcoming high-risk procedure, major trauma, acute ICH).
- **Remove** once no longer needed (typically ~1–2 months {JVIR 2011;22:1522}). Complications: IVC thrombosis, DVT/PE, migration, erosion, fracture.

### Unprovoked VTE
Age-appropriate cancer screening (cancer found in 5% within a year {Annals 2017;167:410}). Limit thrombophilia testing during the acute episode.
