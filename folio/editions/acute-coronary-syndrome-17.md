# Acute Coronary Syndrome
> STEMI → cath lab within 90 minutes (lytics if PCI isn't possible within 120). NSTE-ACS → anticoagulate, then time the cath by risk. Everyone: aspirin, high-intensity statin, a P2Y12 inhibitor, β-blocker and ACEi within 24 h, and no NSAIDs.

## Define
- **Myocardial injury:** troponin >99th percentile without evidence of ischemia (symptoms, new ischemic ECG changes, new wall-motion abnormality, or coronary thrombus). Acute or chronic.
- **Myocardial infarction:** injury (troponin >99th percentile with a rise or fall) **plus** ischemia. Types 1–5. {4th Universal Definition, JACC 2018;72:2231}
  - **Type 1:** plaque rupture or erosion with thrombus
  - **Type 2:** supply–demand mismatch without atherothrombosis: sepsis, arrhythmia, anemia, kidney failure, surgery, HTN, heart failure, vasospasm, microvascular dysfunction (Takotsubo), embolism. {Circulation 2019;140:1661} Needs a clear precipitant; if none, treat as type 1. 50–70% have obstructive CAD, so starting aspirin, a β-blocker and a high-intensity statin is reasonable.

## Troponin pathway (hsTnT)
| | Emergency department, pain began ≥3 h before arrival | Inpatient or ED, pain began <3 h before |
|---|---|---|
| **Check** | Immediately and at 1 h | Immediately and at 3 h |
| **Rule in** | hsTnT ≥52, or rise ≥5 → consider ACS | hsTnT ≥10 (F) / ≥15 (M), and a 3 h rise ≥7, and symptoms, ECG changes or concerning imaging (CCTA, cath) → consider ACS |
| **Rule out** | hsTnT <10 (F) / <12 (M) and rise <3 → ACS unlikely | No significant change in 3 h → ACS unlikely |
| **In between** | Calculate a HEART score; repeat at 3 h and apply the inpatient criteria | |

## Classify
| | STEMI | NSTEMI | Unstable angina |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ECG** | New STE ≥1 mm in 2 contiguous leads (V2–V3: >2.5 mm men <40, >2 mm men ≥40, >1.5 mm women) or new LBBB | ST depression, TWI, flat T waves; up to ⅓ have a normal ECG | Ischemic ECG or history |
| **Troponin** | Positive | **Positive** | **Negative** |

- **Baseline LBBB:** Sgarbossa (≥1 mm concordant STE, ≥1 mm STD in V1–V3, ≥5 mm discordant STE); Smith-modified Sgarbossa and the Barcelona algorithm perform better. {JAHA 2020;19:e017119}
- **Electrically silent territory** (LCx or RCA): posterior leads V7–V9, STE >0.5 mm diagnostic; also tall R in V2–V3, STD in anterior leads (mirror image).
- **Workup and clues:** see *Chest Pain*. Consider risk factors, recent stress test or cath; infection (e.g. influenza) raises MI risk. {NEJM 2018;378:345} Serial ECGs q15–30 min if the first is non-diagnostic with a compelling story. hsTnT 99th percentile: men 15, women 10 ng/L; 75% of healthy people have measurable hsTnT; it peaks at 12–48 h and normalizes in 5–14 days.

## Revascularize
### STEMI
- **Primary PCI** for symptom onset <48 h (door-to-wire goal <60 min at a PCI center)
- **Primary PCI regardless of timing** if cardiogenic shock, recurrent sustained VT/VF, or persistent STE or pain
- **Not after 48 h** in stable patients. {NEJM 2006;355:2395}
- **Fibrinolysis** if PCI isn't possible within 120 min of first medical contact (and onset <24 h): bolus within 10 min (tenecteplase preferred), then transfer immediately to a PCI center. Discuss antithrombotics with cardiology. Contraindications: high bleeding risk (especially prior ICH), suspected dissection, ischemic stroke <3 months, severe HTN.

### NSTE-ACS: how soon to cath {JACC 2014;64:e139}
| Risk | Timing | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Very high** | Immediate (<2 h) | Refractory or recurrent angina, hemodynamic or electrical instability, mechanical complications, recurrent dynamic ECG changes |
| **High** | Early (<24 h) | Rising/falling troponin, transient STE, STD or TWI, GRACE >140 |
| **Intermediate** | Delayed (<72 h) | None of the above but baseline risk (EF <40%, GFR <60) |
| **Low** | Ischemia-guided, no routine cath | No risk factors, GRACE <109, TIMI 0–1, or poor cath candidate |

GRACE predicts 6-month mortality. {BMJ 2006;333:1091} Early cath improves outcomes when GRACE >140 (TIMACS {NEJM 2009;360:2165}, VERDICT {Circ 2018;138:2741}).

### PCI or CABG
Decide by acuity, culprit-lesion anatomy and overall complexity, with a Heart Team when appropriate. {Circulation 2022;145:4} **CABG** is often preferred for 3-vessel disease {NEJM 2009;360:961}, left main {NEJM 2016;375:2223}, 2-vessel with proximal LAD or EF <50%, and diabetes with LAD involvement. {NEJM 2012;367:2375}

**Multivessel PCI:**
- STEMI without shock → complete revascularization (fewer CV deaths and MIs at 3 years; COMPLETE {NEJM 2019;381:1411})
- Any ACS **with shock** → culprit-only PCI (CULPRIT-SHOCK {NEJM 2018;379:1699}); consider staged completion
- NSTE-ACS without shock → complete revascularization (BIOVASC {Lancet 2023;401:1172})

## Medications
### At presentation
| Drug | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspirin** | 325 mg load, then 81 mg daily | Mortality benefit; everyone {Lancet 1988;2:8607} |
| **Anticoagulation** | Start at diagnosis. Stop after PCI unless another indication (AF, PE). If managed medically, usually stop at 48 h once ischemia resolves {BMJ 1996;313:652} | On warfarin/DOAC: plan a short-acting bridge with pharmacy before cath |
| UFH (standard) | Bolus + drip, low-intensity PTT goal 50–70 (or anti-Xa protocol) | No bolus after lytics or if on warfarin with INR <2 |
| LMWH | | Possibly fewer deaths, little extra major bleeding; trials vs UFH mostly neutral {BMJ 2012;344:e553} |
| Fondaparinux | | Preferred if managed medically; **not for PCI** (catheter thrombosis) {JAMA 2006;295:1519} |
| Bivalirudin / argatroban | | Direct thrombin inhibitors; for HIT. Possible benefit over UFH in STEMI {BRIGHT-4, Lancet 2022;400:1847} |
| GP IIb/IIIa (eptifibatide) | In the cath lab | High-risk PCI (heavy thrombus) |
| **Atorvastatin 80 mg** | Daily, regardless of LDL | {NEJM 2004;350:1495} High dose within 24–96 h, before PCI, may reduce death and cardiac events {JACC 2009;54:2157; JAMA 2018;319:1331}. Very high risk with LDL >70 → add ezetimibe, consider PCSK9 inhibitor {JACC 2019;73:3168} |
| **Nitroglycerin** | SL 0.3–0.6 mg ×3; refractory → drip from 5–10 mcg/min, titrated to pain-free | No mortality benefit. Caution: inferior/RV MI, SBP <100, PDE-5 inhibitor within 48 h. Refractory pain → earlier cath |
| **Morphine** | Avoid | Only for intolerable pain despite nitrates; careful with inferior/RV MI |
| **NSAIDs** | **Stop** | More death, reinfarction, HF, rupture |

### Within 24 h or after revascularization
**P2Y12 inhibitor** (timing of pre-loading is debated; it can delay CABG by 5–7 days {Circulation 2022;145:4}):
| Drug | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Ticagrelor** | Lower mortality than clopidogrel without more major bleeding (PLATO {NEJM 2009;361:1045}); reversible with platelets | Dyspnea (14–21%, usually mild), rare alveolar hemorrhage. Avoid with liver disease, prior stroke, oral anticoagulation |
| **Prasugrel** | Fewer deaths, MIs and strokes than ticagrelor {NEJM 2019;381:1524} | **Contraindicated:** prior TIA/stroke, weight <60 kg, age >75 |
| **Clopidogrel** | Less bleeding {Circulation 2020;142:150}; fewer deaths and MIs with PCI {Lancet 2001;358:527} | More ischemic events. Prodrug (CYP2C19); weaker with loss-of-function alleles {NEJM 2009;360:354}. Consider a PPI to prevent GI bleeding {NEJM 2010;363:1909} |
| **Cangrelor** | IV, immediate onset, platelet function back within 1 h | |

- **DAPT:** 12 months by default (DAPT score helps). In select patients, stopping aspirin after 1–3 months (P2Y12 monotherapy) prevents events as well with less bleeding (TWILIGHT, TICO, STOPDAPT-2, SMART CHOICE, GLOBAL LEADERS, MASTER DAPT, HOST-EXAM). Decide with interventional cardiology.
- **Also on long-term anticoagulation** (AF, PE): P2Y12 inhibitor + DOAC beats triple therapy (less bleeding, similar ischemic events). {AUGUSTUS, NEJM 2019;380:1509; RE-DUAL PCI, NEJM 2017;377:1513; NEJM 2016;375:2423; JACC 2021;77:629} Typical: aspirin + clopidogrel + anticoagulant for 1–4 weeks, then stop aspirin.

| Drug | Start | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **β-blocker** | Within 24 h (Class 1B STEMI, 1A NSTE-ACS); earlier if ischemic arrhythmias | Mortality benefit. Not routinely needed long-term after MI with LVEF >50% {NEJM 2024;390:1372}. Caution with decompensated HF or shock risk (age >70, SBP <120, HR >110 or <60). **Contraindicated:** shock, cocaine MI, PR >240 ms, 2nd/3rd-degree AV block, severe bronchospasm {Lancet 2005;366:1622} |
| **ACEi** (ARB if intolerant) | Within 24 h if BP and kidney function allow | Mortality benefit in anterior STEMI; especially HF or LVEF <40% {Circulation 2008;117:269} |
| **MRA** | After MI on ACEi + β-blocker, with LVEF ≤40% plus diabetes or HF symptoms | |

**Lifestyle:** stop smoking; BP <130/80 (especially with diabetes or CKD); cardiac rehab; screen for depression; influenza vaccine.
