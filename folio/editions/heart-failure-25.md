# Heart Failure
> Name the profile (warm/cold, wet/dry), find the trigger, decongest with IV loop diuretics at 2–2.5× the home dose, and send HFrEF patients home on all four GDMT pillars. {AHA/ACC/HFSA 2022, Circ 2022;145:895}

## Definitions
- **Heart failure** is clinical: congestion (orthopnea, edema, crackles, high JVP) from impaired ventricular filling or ejection.
- **Cardiomyopathy** is structural/functional heart muscle disease (usually seen on echo) and may exist without congestion.

| Class | LVEF |
|---|---|
| **HFrEF** | ≤40% |
| **HFmrEF** (mildly reduced) | 41–49% |
| **HFpEF** | ≥50% |
| **HFimpEF** (improved) | Previously ≤40%, now >40% |

- **ACC/AHA stage:** A at risk · B structural disease, no symptoms · C symptomatic HF · D advanced, refractory
- **NYHA class:** I no limitation · II symptoms with ordinary activity · III symptoms with less than ordinary activity · IV symptoms at rest
- Morphology: dilated, hypertrophic, restrictive.

## Admitted with decompensated HF
### 1. Profile and triage {JACC 2019;74:1966}
| | Dry | Wet |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** | Compensated | **Most common.** Diurese |
| **Cold** (AMS, high lactate, cool extremities, narrow pulse pressure) | Low output; cautious volume, consider inotropes | Diurese + support output; consider CCU |

- The CXR can miss pulmonary edema (lymphatic compensation). {Chest 2004;125:669} Primarily right-sided disease: see *Right Ventricular Failure*.
- **Step-down/higher level if:** EF <25%, NT-proBNP ≥2,500, or arrhythmia-driven HF.
- Black and Latinx patients with HF are less often admitted to cardiology services, contributing to worse outcomes. {Circ Heart Fail 2019;12:e006214}

### 2. Find the trigger: FAILURE
**F**orgot meds · **A**rrhythmia, anemia, afterload (HTN) · **I**nfection, ischemia · **L**ifestyle (salt) · **U**pregulated output (pregnancy, hyperthyroidism) · **R**enal failure · **E**mbolism. Also: progression, too little diuretic, NSAIDs, steroids, CCBs, anthracyclines, alcohol, new or worse valve disease.

Get the HF history: prior diagnosis, dry weight, last NT-proBNP and echo, ischemic work-up, outpatient cardiologist, accurate med list.

### 3. Orders
- Telemetry; 2 g Na diet; daily standing weights; strict I/O; VTE prophylaxis; electrolyte replacement scales (step-down)
- **NT-proBNP and weight on admission.** ADHF unlikely if NT-proBNP <300 (NPV 98%); likely if >450 (>900 if age >50). {Am J Cardiol 2005;95:948} Hard to read in CKD/dialysis; falsely low in obesity and HFpEF.
- **Avoid:** calcium channel blockers (especially diltiazem/verapamil), NSAIDs, flecainide.
- **Iron:** check in everyone regardless of Hgb {JACC HF 2019;7:36}. Deficient if ferritin <100, or <300 with TSat <20% {JACC 2017;70:776}. Replete IV: better symptoms and exercise capacity, possibly outcomes. {JACC 2018;71:782; NEJM 2023;389:975}

### 4. Decongest {NEJM 2017;377:1964; JACC 2020;75:1178}
:::key Diuresis
- **Start:** IV loop diuretic. Diuretic-naive: furosemide 20–40 mg IV. On diuretics: 2–2.5× the home dose (IV). Bolus = continuous drip. {NEJM 2011;364:797}
- **Not enough:** add metolazone 2.5–5 mg (or chlorothiazide 500 mg IV) 30 min before the loop. Acetazolamide 500 mg daily or dapagliflozin 10 mg daily can help. {NEJM 2022;387:1185; EHJ 2023;44:2966} Still refractory → RHC or inotropes. Stepped drug therapy beats ultrafiltration in cardiorenal syndrome. {NEJM 2012;367:2296} See *Advanced Diuresis*.
- **Creatinine bumps** are common and usually benign; keep going if still congested. {Circ 2018;137:2016}
- **Endpoint:** no congestion. Use weights, hemoconcentration, JVP and lung POCUS (B-lines predict readmission and death). {EHJ 2016;37:1244}
:::
- **Acute pulmonary edema:** NIPPV may lower mortality and intubation. {Annals 2010;152:590}
- **Vasodilators** (lower afterload and PCWP), especially with severe HTN or acute MR/AR. Floor: isosorbide dinitrate, hydralazine, nitropaste, captopril. Step-down/CCU: nitroglycerin or nitroprusside drip (caution with uncorrected CAD).
- **Home GDMT:** unless in cardiogenic shock or new AKI, continue ACEi/ARB/ARNI and β-blocker (fractionated); don't start a new β-blocker during decompensation. {EHJ 2009;30:2186} Hold SGLT2i if a procedure is likely.

## GDMT
### HFrEF / HFmrEF / HFimpEF: four pillars
Start all four at low doses before discharge, then titrate.
| | Drug | Start | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **β-blocker** (COPERNICUS, MERIT-HF) | Metoprolol succinate | 12.5–25 mg daily | 200 mg daily |
| | Carvedilol | 3.125 mg BID | 25–50 mg BID |
| **ARNI / ACEi / ARB** (PARADIGM-HF, PIONEER-HF; CONSENSUS, CHARM) | Sacubitril/valsartan | 49/51 mg BID | 97/103 mg BID |
| | Lisinopril | 2.5–5 mg daily | 20–40 mg daily |
| | Losartan | 25–50 mg daily | 50–150 mg daily |
| **MRA** (RALES, EMPHASIS-HF) | Spironolactone | 12.5–25 mg daily | 25–50 mg daily |
| | Eplerenone | 25 mg daily | 50 mg daily |
| **SGLT2i** (DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced) | Dapagliflozin | 10 mg daily | 10 mg daily |
| | Empagliflozin | 10 mg daily | 10 mg daily |

- **β-blocker:** caution after recent inotropes.
- **ARNI** preferred if kidney function is stable; switching from an ACEi needs a **36 h washout**.
- **MRA** only if CrCl >30. Check K and Cr within 72 h of discharge (hyperkalemia as diuretics are cut back).
- **SGLT2i** lower death and HF admissions with or without diabetes.
- Advanced CKD: {JACC HF 2019;7:371}

### HFpEF
- Diurese, and treat diabetes, HTN and AF.
- **SGLT2i** lower CV death and HF admissions with EF >40% (EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER).
- **Spironolactone** if kidney function and K allow (benefit in the Americas cohort of TOPCAT). {Circ 2015;131:34}
- **Newer:** finerenone {NEJM 2024;391:1475}; semaglutide in obesity. {NEJM 2023;389:2221}
- No proven benefit: β-blockers {EHJ 2018;39:26}, ACEi (PEP-CHF), ARNI (PARAGON-HF), ARB (CHARM-Preserved, I-PRESERVE).

### Devices {JACC 2013;61:e6; EHJ 2016;37:2129}
- **ICD:** ischemic CM with EF ≤30%, or ≤35% with NYHA II–III
- **CRT:** EF ≤35% with a wide QRS ± LBBB (some with EF ≤50%). See *Cardiac Devices*.

## Before discharge
- Document discharge weight and NT-proBNP; HF transitions clinic appointment; cardiac rehab referral
- GDMT started and titrated as above
- **Diuretic plan:** oral maintenance dose, when to take extra rescue doses, whether K is needed. Consider watching on the oral dose before discharge.

## Cardiogenic shock (CCU)
**SCAI stages** {JSCAI 2021;1:1}: A at risk · B beginning (hemodynamic instability, no hypoperfusion) · C classic (hypoperfusion: oliguria, high lactate or Cr; BP may be normal) · D deteriorating despite therapy · E extremis.

- **Evaluate:** ECG and troponin (acute MI?); echo for tamponade, mechanical complications and MCS contraindications
- **Monitor:** arterial line; consider a PA catheter (see *Pulmonary Artery Catheterization*)
- **Acute MI → cath lab:** revascularization is the only intervention proven to improve outcomes. {NEJM 1999;341:625}
- **Norepinephrine** to stabilize MAP until hemodynamics guide therapy. Escalating pressors predicts poor outcomes; there is growing evidence for early MCS (see *Mechanical Circulatory Support*).

### Tailored therapy (PA catheter)
| Target | Goal | Tools |
|---|---|---|
| **Preload** (PCWP ≈ LVEDP) | PCWP 14–18, PA diastolic 16–20, CVP 8–12 | Diuresis; ultrafiltration with RRT |
| **Afterload** (SVR = (MAP − CVP)/CO) | MAP >60, SVR <800–1200 | Vasodilators: captopril, hydralazine, nitroprusside, nitroglycerin, isosorbide dinitrate. Pressors if needed for mixed shock or to offset inodilators |
| **Contractility** | CO >4, CI >2.0–2.2, mixed venous O2 >65% | Inotropes (below) |

| Inotrope | Start | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dobutamine** (β1 > β2) | 0.5–1 mcg/kg/min | Tachycardia, faster AF, arrhythmias, ischemia, hypotension; tachyphylaxis after 24–48 h |
| **Milrinone** (PDE-3 inhibitor) | 0.125 mcg/kg/min | Longer half-life, more pulmonary vasodilation, a bit less tachycardia. **Preferred in RV failure.** Renally cleared. Common choice for palliative home inotropes |
| **Epinephrine** | | Severe hypotension, often with an inodilator; arrhythmias, organ hypoperfusion |

## Find the cause
| Category | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Most common** | Ischemic, hypertensive, valvular |
| **Metabolic** | Diabetic, nutritional (beriberi) |
| **High-output** | Sepsis, pregnancy (peripartum), hyperthyroidism, anemia, liver failure, Paget's, AV shunts |
| **Toxins / drugs** | Alcohol, cocaine/amphetamines, chemotherapy, checkpoint inhibitors |
| **Infection** | HIV, coxsackie, echovirus, EBV, CMV, Lyme, Chagas, TB |
| **Infiltrative** | Amyloidosis (ATTR, AL), hemochromatosis, sarcoidosis, Löffler |
| **Genetic** | HCM, ARVC, neuromuscular disorders, glycogen storage disease, LV non-compaction |
| **Inflammatory** | Connective tissue disease, giant cell myocarditis |
| **Other** | Cor pulmonale, Takotsubo, tachycardia-induced, pericardial disease (constriction, effusion), radiation, carcinoid, idiopathic |

- **Everyone:** congestion exam, troponin, NT-proBNP, ECG, telemetry, CXR, echo
- **Ischemic work-up:** family and smoking history, lipids, A1c; see *Non-Invasive Cardiac Testing* and *Cardiac Catheterization*
- **Non-ischemic:** family, medication and substance history; tox screen, β-hCG, iron studies, SPEP/UPEP/free light chains, TSH, HIV, ANA
- **Extended:** chest CT, cardiac MRI, RHC, endomyocardial biopsy, PYP scan, viral, Lyme and T. cruzi serologies, ACE, thiamine/carnitine/selenium, genetics

**Cause-specific treatment:** ischemic → PCI/CABG; valvular → TAVR, MitraClip, other valve procedures; tachycardia-induced → ablation, pacing, CRT; **ATTR amyloid** → tafamidis {NEJM 2018;379:1007}, acoramidis, vutrisiran; sarcoid → prednisone and steroid-sparing therapy.

### Specific cardiomyopathies
::: Hypertrophic (HCM) | LVH ± dynamic LVOT obstruction {Circulation 2024;149:e1239}
- **Features:** LV/RV hypertrophy, LVOT obstruction (HOCM), diastolic dysfunction, ischemia, MR, sudden death risk
- **Exam:** systolic murmur at the left lower sternal border/apex that **gets louder with Valsalva or standing**; paradoxical S2 split; S4
- **Diagnose:** ECG (high voltage, deep inferolateral Q waves, LAD; deep TWI V2–V4 in apical HCM); echo (wall >15 mm, SAM, outflow gradient ± provocation); cardiac MRI (LGE = fibrosis). Genetic testing (mutation in ~70%) helps screen family, not diagnose or risk-stratify.
- **Treat:** avoid volume depletion and vasodilators. **Hypotension not responding to fluid → phenylephrine.** Activity guidance; β-blocker > verapamil; disopyramide. **Myosin inhibitors** (mavacamten, aficamten) if LVEF ≥55% (VALOR-HCM). Septal ablation or myectomy if refractory. ICD if high risk.
- **Sudden death risk:** prior VT/arrest or unexplained syncope; family history of sudden death; wall ≥30 mm; NSVT; abnormal BP response to exercise; extensive LGE; EF <50%
:::
::: Amyloid, hemochromatosis, sarcoid | Clues
- **Amyloidosis (AL, ATTR):** HF plus other organ involvement (e.g. kidney); **low-voltage ECG despite thick walls**; subendocardial LGE
- **Hemochromatosis:** SVT, dilated LV; iron on T2* cardiac MRI
- **Sarcoidosis:** pseudo-infarct ECG or heart block; patchy basal LGE
:::
