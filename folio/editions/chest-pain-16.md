# Chest Pain
> Rule out the killers first: ACS, aortic dissection, PE, tamponade, tension pneumothorax, esophageal rupture. A 12-lead ECG within minutes, troponin, CXR, and a focused exam (pulses in both arms) sort most patients.

## First 10 minutes
:::key Do now
1. **ECG** and compare with prior; repeat q10–15 min if suspicious. A single ECG is <50% sensitive for acute MI. Check posterior leads if STD in V1–3 or R>S in V1–2; right-sided leads if STE in II/III/aVF (for RCA/RV). With LBBB or paced rhythm, use Sgarbossa criteria.
2. **High-sensitivity troponin,** repeated (see *Acute Coronary Syndrome*)
3. **CXR**
4. **Exam:** BP in both arms, pulses, murmurs, JVP, lungs, calves
:::
| ECG / first findings | Think | Next |
|---|---|---|
| New STE ≥1 mm in 2 contiguous leads (V2–3: men >2, women >1.5), or new LBBB | **STEMI** | Cath lab now |
| STD ≥0.5 mm or TWI | **NSTE-ACS** | Serial troponin → ACS pathway |
| Diffuse upsloping STE, PR depression, PR elevation in aVR, high CRP | **Pericarditis** | Echo for effusion |
| Sinus tachycardia, RAD, new RBBB, TWI V1–4, S1Q3T3 (specific, not sensitive) | **PE** (RV strain) | CT-PE; consider valve disease or cardiomyopathy |
| Non-diagnostic ECG → CXR shows pneumothorax or pneumonia | PTX / PNA | Treat |
| CXR wide mediastinum | **Aortic dissection** | CTA chest |
| Normal CXR and ECG | Non-cardiopulmonary causes | See differential |

## Differential
| Cause | History | Exam |
|---|---|---|
| **ACS / stable angina** {Circulation 2021;22;e368} | **Classic triad:** (1) substernal pressure, (2) worse with exertion, (3) relieved by rest or nitroglycerin. **Equivalents:** chest tightness; shoulder, arm, neck, back, upper abdominal or jaw pain; dyspnea, fatigue, nausea, presyncope. Women, older adults and people with diabetes more often have less classic symptoms. Describe as cardiac / possibly cardiac / noncardiac, not "typical/atypical" {Circulation 2022;80(17)} | Distress, diaphoresis; new S4 or MR (ischemia); heart failure (crackles, S3, JVD, edema); bruits (vascular disease); document femoral and radial pulses before cath. Exam is often normal. Pleuritic, positional or reproducible pain makes ACS less likely (LR 0.28) |
| **Acute aortic syndrome** | Abrupt tearing or sharp chest or abdominal pain. Risk: known aneurysm, Marfan or other connective tissue disease, HTN, male (2:1), age 60–80, cocaine, intense exertion (weightlifting) | Arm BP differs by >20, pulse deficits, new diastolic murmur, focal neuro signs |
| **Pericarditis** | Sharp, pleuritic, better leaning forward; viral prodrome; rheumatologic history; high fever → think bacterial | Friction rub; tamponade (pulsus >10) |
| **PE** | Sudden dyspnea, hypoxemia, pleuritic pain. Risk: cancer, recent surgery, immobility; hemoptysis; calf or thigh pain/swelling | Tachycardia, tachypnea, hypoxemia, leg swelling/tenderness, RV strain |
| **Pneumothorax** | Sudden dyspnea. Risk: age 20–40 (tall), personal or family history, smoking, emphysema, male, recent chest procedures or lines | Absent breath sounds on one side; tracheal deviation away (tension) |
| **Pneumonia / pneumonitis** | Sharp pleuritic pain with fever, high WBC, productive cough; recent radiation; autoimmune disease (SLE, RA, drug-induced lupus) | Bronchial breath sounds, crackles, dullness |
| **Other** | **Cardiac:** HOCM, AS, vasospasm (Prinzmetal, drugs), Takotsubo. **MSK:** costochondritis, zoster. **GI:** GERD, esophageal spasm (may respond to nitroglycerin), Boerhaave, ulcer, biliary colic, pancreatitis. **Psych:** panic attack | |

### Likelihood ratios for ACS {JAMA 2015;314:1955}
| Finding | LR |
|---|---|
| Pleuritic pain | 0.3 |
| Syncope | 0.5 |
| Radiation to left arm, neck or jaw | 1.3–1.5 |
| Diaphoresis | 1.4 |
| Exertional | 1.5 |
| Pressure / classic | 1.9 |
| Change in pattern within 24 h | 2.0 |

| PAD | 2.7 |

## Testing
| Test | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stress test** | Resolved or stable pain; low–intermediate risk | Rules out ACS on the low end |
| **Coronary CTA** | Same group, especially age <65 | ~100% NPV without known CAD {JAMA Cardiol 2020;5:193} |
| **Angiography** | STEMI (emergent), high-risk NSTEMI (early), all confirmed ACS | Risk-stratify with GRACE, TIMI, HEART |
| **POCUS / echo** | RV strain (D sign, McConnell sign), valves, EF | For PE, echo is specific but not sensitive {J Am Soc Echo 2017;30:714} |
| **TEE** | Prosthetic mitral valve; suspected proximal dissection (root, valve) | |
| **CT-PE** | Suspected PE | NPV 60% / 89% / 96% for high / intermediate / low pretest probability (PIOPED II) |
| **CTA chest** | Suspected dissection | Sn 97–100%, Sp 83–100% {NEJM 1993;328:35} |

## Management pearls
- **Antianginals:** nitrates (avoid if preload-dependent: RV infarct, hypotension, AS, recent PDE-5 inhibitor); β-blocker (caution in decompensated HF, long PR, 2nd/3rd-degree AV block); calcium channel blocker if β-blocker isn't tolerated.
- Significant racial, ethnic and gender disparities exist in diagnosing and treating ACS. {Circulation 2023;43:1369}
