# Stroke & TIA
> Time is brain: check glucose, get a CT/CTA, and treat eligible patients with tenecteplase within 4.5 h and thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusion up to 24 h. Then find the mechanism and start the right secondary prevention, usually short-course DAPT for minor stroke or high-risk TIA.

## Acute ischemic stroke
:::key Thrombolysis
**Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (max 25 mg)** is now preferred over alteplase.
- **0–3 h from last seen well:** start within 60 min of arrival. {AHA/ASA, Stroke 2018;49:e46}
- **3–4.5 h:** recommended; relative exclusions are age >80, any anticoagulant use (regardless of INR), NIHSS >25, and prior stroke plus diabetes. {NEJM 2008;359:1317}
- **Wake-up or unknown onset:** consider if the DWI lesion is <70 mL with no FLAIR change. {WAKE-UP, NEJM 2018;379:611}
:::
| Eligible if | Not if |
|---|---|
| Severe or disabling deficit; age ≥18; within 4.5 h (or wake-up criteria) | **History:** stroke or head trauma <3 months; head or spine surgery <3 months; prior ICH; intracranial tumor, AVM or aneurysm; non-compressible arterial puncture <7 days; GI cancer or GI bleed <21 days |
| | **Clinical:** BP ≥185/110 that can't be lowered; glucose <50; active internal bleeding or bleeding diathesis |
| | **Labs:** platelets <100K, PTT >40 s, PT >15 s; warfarin with INR >1.7; therapeutic heparin within 48 h with high PTT; DOAC within 48 h |
| | **CT:** hemorrhage or an established infarct |

### Thrombectomy
- **Proven benefit:** NIHSS ≥6, within 24 h, age 18–90, baseline mRS ≤2, life expectancy >12 months, **and** ICA or M1/M2 occlusion (or basilar/vertebral) with a small core or favorable mismatch. {NEJM 2015;372:11; NEJM 2018;378:708; NEJM 2023;388:1259}
- **Probable benefit:** NIHSS ≥4, mRS ≤3, proximal occlusion with ASPECTS ≥5. Discuss every case with neurology.
- **Within 6 h** for disabling deficits with proximal LVO; **6–24 h** by perfusion imaging or clinical mismatch. No benefit for medium or distal occlusions. {NEJM 2025}
- **Not if:** contrast anaphylaxis, acute ICH.

### Blood pressure
| Situation | Goal |
|---|---|
| **Before thrombolysis** | ≤185/110: treat immediately (IV labetalol or nicardipine) |
| **After thrombolysis** | ≤180/105 for 24 h |
| **No thrombolysis** | ≤220/120 for 24 h (permissive), then lower gradually |
| **Anticoagulated** | SBP ≤180 (hemorrhagic transformation) |
| **ACS needing tighter control** | Discuss with neurology |

- SBP <150 is linked to worse outcomes. {Arch Intern Med 2003;163:211}
- **Worse with lower BP?** Possible BP-dependent ischemia: lay the bed flat, give a fluid bolus, call neurology STAT.
- **Hemorrhagic stroke:** see *CNS Emergencies*.

:::tip Stroke mimics: MINT
**M**etabolic (glucose, Na, uremia, toxic-metabolic encephalopathy, thyroid, MELAS, hypoxia, hypercarbia) · **I**nfection (Bell's palsy, meningitis, encephalitis, vestibular neuritis) · **N**eurologic (migraine, Todd's paralysis, functional disorder, MS, MOGAD, NMO) · **T**oxins, trauma
:::

## After the acute phase
- **Neuro checks** q1–2h × 24 h if unstable/ICU, q4h if stable; **STAT head CT** for any change
- **PT, OT, speech;** NPO until a bedside swallow screen
- **Normothermia** (antipyretics) and **glucose <180**
- **After thrombolysis:** no antithrombotics for 24 h; head CT at 24 h; if no bleed, start VTE prophylaxis
- **No thrombolysis:** aspirin 325 mg once, then long-term antiplatelet or anticoagulant (may be delayed for large strokes); VTE prophylaxis unless large hemorrhagic conversion

### Work-up
- **Labs:** lipids, A1c, TSH, ESR, CRP, CYP2C19 genotype. Young or cryptogenic: syphilis, HIV, infection work-up, ± thrombophilia (with neurology). {Stroke 2021;52:364}
- **Imaging:** MRI brain without contrast; CTA or MRA head and neck (time-of-flight MRA if low GFR). CTA/MRA show intracranial vessels better than carotid ultrasound.
- **Heart:** TTE (bubble if <60); telemetry, then 30-day monitor or loop recorder if no AF is known.

## Secondary prevention
Decide with neurology (mechanism, infarct size, NIHSS, ABCD2). Usually **not** both antiplatelet and anticoagulant: anticoagulate if there's an indication; otherwise DAPT if started within 24–48 h for minor stroke/high-risk TIA (benefit is in the first 3 weeks).
- **High-intensity statin** (atorvastatin 40–80 mg), LDL <70. {NEJM 2006;355; NEJM 2020;382:9}

| Situation | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Minor stroke (NIHSS <4) or high-risk TIA (ABCD2 ≥4)** | Aspirin 81 mg + clopidogrel 300–600 mg load, then aspirin 81 + clopidogrel 75 mg × 3 weeks, then one agent. {CHANCE, NEJM 2013;369:11; POINT, NEJM 2018;379} Ticagrelor can replace clopidogrel (faster, not CYP2C19-dependent; loss-of-function alleles are common in people of Han Chinese descent) {NEJM 2020;383:207; NEJM 2021;385:2520} |
| **Larger or cryptogenic stroke** | Aspirin 325 mg load, then 81 mg daily (50–325 mg works; ≤200 mg bleeds less) {Am J Cardiol 2005;95:1218} |
| **Atherosclerotic disease** | Clopidogrel 75 mg may beat aspirin {Lancet 1996;348:1329} |
| **Intracranial atherosclerosis** | Aspirin 325 + clopidogrel 75 × 3 months, then aspirin 325 {NEJM 2011;365:993} |
| **Recurrence on one antiplatelet** | Long-term DAPT bleeds more without clear benefit {NEJM 2006;354:1706; Lancet 2004;364:331}; consider aspirin + low-dose rivaroxaban {NEJM 2017;377} |
| **Cardioembolic** (AF, paradoxical embolus, LV thrombus, venous sinus thrombosis, hypercoagulable) | **Anticoagulate.** DOAC > warfarin (warfarin for moderate–severe MS). Start immediately after TIA; 1–2 days for small, 3–4 for moderate, 5–7 for large infarcts {NEJM 2023;388:2411}. Hemorrhagic transformation → discuss timing. LAA occlusion if anticoagulation isn't possible {JACC 2020;76:2795} |

## By mechanism
| Mechanism | Suspect when | Work-up | Treat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cardioembolic** | Cortical occlusion without vascular disease; multiple territories; AF, LA/LV thrombus, EF <35%, arch atheroma, LA dilation, right-to-left shunt; hypercoagulable states (cancer, sickle cell, cryoglobulins) | TTE (bubble if <60). **PFO:** look for venous thrombus (leg Dopplers, pelvic MRV); consider closure {NEJM 2017;377:1022}. No known AF: telemetry, then 30-day monitor or loop recorder (significance uncertain) {NEJM 2014;370:2478; JAMA 2021;325:2169} | Anticoagulate at 2–7 days unless hemorrhagic transformation |
| **Symptomatic carotid stenosis** | Amaurosis fugax; ipsilateral >50% (NASCET), or <50% with high-risk plaque (intraplaque hemorrhage, ulceration) {Stroke 2020;311-314} | CTA or MRA; carotid ultrasound before endarterectomy | Revascularize (CEA or stent) **within 2 weeks** {NEJM 1998;339:1415}. Discuss temporary anticoagulation. Induced HTN if symptoms fluctuate with BP |
| **Endocarditis** | Fever with stroke, or valve disease | Blood cultures; TTE then TEE or PET; CTA ± angiography for mycotic aneurysm | Antibiotics now. **No thrombolysis.** Early surgery if small non-hemorrhagic stroke; delay 2–4 weeks if large or hemorrhagic. No antithrombotics without another indication {Stroke 2011;42:1797} |
| **Carotid or vertebral dissection** | Age <60 or posterior stroke without risk factors; neck pain, headache, Horner; trauma, chiropractic, coughing | CTA or MRA with T1 fat saturation; consider connective tissue disease (Marfan, FMD) | Risk is highest in the first days. **Anticoagulate** for intraluminal thrombus or occlusion if bleeding risk is low; **antiplatelet** for intracranial extension (SAH risk), large infarct or high bleeding risk {Stroke 2024;91-106}. Treat 3–6 months, then re-image |
| **Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis** | Positional headache, vomiting, papilledema, vision change; seizures (common, hard to control) | CT (dense delta sign); CTV or MRV; consider thrombophilia work-up | **Anticoagulate immediately (heparin or LMWH), even with hemorrhage.** AEDs only for seizures. Fluids; stop smoking and estrogen. Warfarin/DOAC 3–6 months, then re-image {JAMA Neurol 2019;76:1457} |

## TIA
Transient focal brain, spinal cord or retinal ischemia **without** infarction on imaging. Same risk factors as stroke.
- **CT/CTA head and neck** urgently; outpatient MRI may be reasonable from the ED
- **TTE** (bubble if <60, or ≥60 without vascular risk factors); rhythm monitoring if embolic
- **ABCD2** (Age, BP, Clinical features, Duration, Diabetes) predicts stroke within 1 week
- Stroke risk is 1.5–3.5% within 48 h, and immediate treatment lowers it. **ABCD2 ≥4 → DAPT** (unless anticoagulation is indicated).

## After discharge
- Follow pending labs (thrombophilia; repeat antiphospholipid antibodies at 12 weeks if positive), rhythm monitoring and repeat imaging
- **Stop short-term DAPT at 21 days;** start anticoagulation per neurology
- Screen for **depression** {Br J Psychiatry 2013;202:14}; fluoxetine may improve motor recovery {Lancet Neurol 2011;10:123}
- Deficits can keep improving for >1 year. Smoking cessation, diet, exercise, weight loss; sleep study if OSA is suspected.
