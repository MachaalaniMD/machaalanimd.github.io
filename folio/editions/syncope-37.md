# Syncope
> Brief loss of consciousness from global cerebral hypoperfusion, with full spontaneous recovery. History and ECG make most diagnoses. The job is to find the cardiac causes; most syncope is reflex or orthostatic. {AHA/ACC/HRS, JACC 2017;70:e39}

## Is it syncope?
- **Definition:** transient loss of consciousness and postural tone from cerebral hypoperfusion, with complete spontaneous recovery
- **Not syncope:** seizure, metabolic (hypoglycemia, hypoxia), intoxication or meds, vertebrobasilar TIA, mechanical fall, psychogenic, autonomic failure

## Who needs admission?
:::warn High risk
- **Symptoms:** palpitations before, exertional, while supine, no prodrome, with bleeding or trauma
- **Background:** angina, heart failure, moderate–severe valve or structural heart disease, ischemic or arrhythmic ECG, family history of sudden death, pre-excitation, high-risk job (e.g. pilot), facial injury (no warning)
:::
- **San Francisco Syncope Rule:** admit if any of ECG changes or non-sinus rhythm, dyspnea, Hct <30, SBP <90, history of heart failure.
- Risk scores have NPV >95% but don't replace judgment.

## Causes
::: Reflex (60%) | Vasovagal, situational, carotid sinus
- **Vasovagal:** prodrome of lightheadedness, nausea, warmth, sweating, pallor; triggered by emotion, pain, stress or prolonged standing
- **Situational:** cough, sneeze, laugh, urination, defecation, after exercise
- **Carotid sinus:** neck turning, prior neck surgery or radiation
- **Diagnose:** history is enough for vasovagal; tilt-table only if unclear. {JACC 1996;28:263} Carotid sinus massage for carotid sinus syncope (not with bruits or prior stroke).
- **Treat:** avoid triggers; counterpressure maneuvers (leg crossing, hand grip, arm tensing, squatting); midodrine, fludrocortisone or β-blocker for select patients. {NEJM 2005;352:1004}
:::
::: Orthostatic (15%) | Autonomic failure, drugs, volume loss
- **Autonomic failure:** primary (Parkinson's, Lewy body, MSA) or secondary (diabetes, amyloid, spinal cord injury, chronic alcohol, B12 deficiency)
- **Drugs:** vasodilators, diuretics, β-blockers, TCAs, Parkinson meds, opioids, α-blockers
- **Diagnose:** orthostatic vitals: SBP falls ≥20 or DBP ≥10 within 3 min of standing (or tilt ≥60°). {Circ EP 2022;15:3} A rising HR is not part of the definition. Variant: a smaller but symptomatic fall from a low supine SBP (90–100). Consider Hct, A1c, SPEP (amyloid), B12.
- **Treat secondary causes:** volume, stop culprit drugs. **Primary:** fludrocortisone 0.05–0.3 mg daily, midodrine 2.5–10 mg BID–TID, pyridostigmine, droxidopa (Parkinson's).
:::
::: Cardiac (15%) | Arrhythmia, structural, obstruction, dissection
- **Clues:** no prodrome, syncope sitting or supine, palpitations, personal or family history of heart disease
- **Monitoring** by frequency: telemetry, Holter, Zio, implantable loop recorder
- **TTE** only if history or exam suggests cardiac disease (<1% yield with normal ECG and no heart disease). ROMEO criteria: Sn 99.5%, Sp 15.4%. {JHM 2018;13:823}
- **PE:** found in 17.3% of patients hospitalized with a first syncope (25.4% when no other cause was apparent). {NEJM 2016;375:1524}
- **Treat:** VT → antiarrhythmic or ablation, consider ICD; SVT with an accessory pathway → ablation; bradyarrhythmia → pacemaker depending on cause; AS → AVR once other causes are excluded
:::
::: Neurologic (<10%) | Seizure, stroke/TIA, subclavian steal
- **Seizure clues:** lateral tongue bite (Sn 20–33%, Sp 96–100%), incontinence (Sn 38%, Sp 57%), aura, postictal confusion. Brief myoclonus is common after any syncope.
- **Tests:** EEG (seizure); CT, MRI/MRA (stroke); upper-extremity arterial Dopplers (subclavian steal: syncope after arm exercise). **Carotid Dopplers rarely help** (change management in <2%). {JAHA 2014;3:e001063}
- Neurology consult by cause.
:::

### Cardiac syncope in young people: check the ECG
| Condition | ECG clue |
|---|---|
| WPW | Delta wave |
| HOCM | LVH, apical TWI |
| Brugada | Pseudo-RBBB with coved or saddleback ST in V1–V2 |
| Long QT | QTc >500 |
| ARVC | Epsilon wave |
| CPVT | Normal ECG; diagnose with exercise test |
