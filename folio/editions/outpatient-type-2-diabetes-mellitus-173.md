# Outpatient Type 2 Diabetes
> Screen from age 35. Diagnose with A1c ≥6.5% (confirmed). Aim for A1c <7% (looser if frail). Start metformin; add an SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 RA for heart and kidney benefit; use a GLP-1 RA before mealtime insulin. {ADA Standards of Care 2025}

## Screening {Diab Care 2025;48:S35}
- **Everyone from age 35.** Repeat every 3 years if normal, yearly if prediabetic.
- **Screen earlier** (any age) if any of these:
  1. BMI ≥25 (≥23 in Asian Americans) **plus** a risk factor: first-degree relative with type 2 diabetes, non-white race/ethnicity, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, HDL <35, triglycerides >250, PCOS, sedentary lifestyle, acanthosis nigricans, MASLD
  2. History of gestational diabetes (screen every 1–3 years) or pancreatitis
  3. HIV, before starting or switching ART

## Diagnose {Diab Care 2025;48:S27}
| Test | Prediabetes | Diabetes |
|---|---|---|
| **A1c** | 5.7–6.4% | ≥6.5% |
| **Fasting glucose** | 100–125 | ≥126 |
| **2 h glucose, 75 g OGTT** | 140–199 | ≥200 |
| **Random glucose** | | ≥200 **with symptoms** |

- **Confirm** with a repeat or second test (same sample or soon after), unless symptoms plus random glucose >200.
- **Use glucose, not A1c,** when red-cell turnover is abnormal: sickle cell disease, 2nd/3rd trimester pregnancy, G6PD deficiency, hemodialysis, HIV, recent blood loss or transfusion, EPO. A1c is also unreliable postpartum, on some HIV drugs, with thalassemia and with anemia.
- **Type 1:** TSH at diagnosis and yearly; screen for thyroid disease and celiac at diagnosis and if symptoms develop. {Diab Care 2025;48:S63}

### Prediabetes
- **Monitor:** A1c at least yearly (every 6 months if 6–6.4%); when starting a second-generation antipsychotic and 12–16 weeks later; before starting or switching ART and 3–6 months after.
- **Treat:** lifestyle change works best; metformin also works, especially if BMI ≥35, age <60, or prior gestational diabetes. {Cochrane Rev 2019}

## Targets
| | Goal |
|---|---|
| **A1c** | <7%; relax to <8–8.5% if life expectancy ≤10 years or high hypoglycemia risk (e.g. ESRD) |
| **Fasting / pre-meal glucose** | 80–130 |
| **1–2 h after meals** | <180 |
| **Blood pressure** | <130/80 (ACEi/ARB first); <120/70 if multiple CV risk factors and safely achievable |
| **LDL** | <70 if >1 ASCVD risk factor; <55 with established ASCVD |

## Routine care {Diab Care 2024;47:S56, S116, S182; Diab Care 2025;48:S59-85}
::: Every visit | Review and examine
- Glucose log (fasting 80–130, post-meal <180); frequency, causes and timing of hypoglycemia
- Medications and how they're actually taken; tobacco, alcohol and substance use, with counseling
- BP (goal above)
- Weight/BMI: nutrition referral for everyone; weight center if BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with poor control
- Foot exam (skin, joints, pulses, sensation), especially with neuropathy or PAD; ABIs/vascular referral if PAD
- Skin: acanthosis nigricans, injection or device sites, lipodystrophy
:::
::: Every 3–6 months | A1c
Every 6 months if at goal on a stable regimen; every 3 months if above goal, after a change, with hypo/hyperglycemia or a change in health.
:::
::: Every year | Labs and screening
- **Lipids / statin:** age 20–39 consider a statin with other risk factors; **age 40–75 moderate-intensity statin for all**, regardless of LDL. >1 ASCVD risk factor → high intensity, LDL <70. Clinical ASCVD (ACS, TIA) → LDL <55 with a statin plus other agents.
- **Kidneys:** urine albumin/Cr and eGFR. ACEi/ARB if hypertensive with albuminuria or GFR <60. Nephrology if GFR <30.
- **Neuropathy:** 10 g monofilament (abnormal if no sensation at 4 of 10 sites), plus pinprick, vibration or reflexes
- **Eyes:** dilated exam or retinal photos; every 2–3 years if repeatedly normal
- **Liver:** if LFTs are high, elastography and/or hepatology for MASLD
- **B12** if on metformin; **TSH** yearly in type 1
:::
::: Vaccines | Per CDC
Influenza yearly, COVID-19, hepatitis B series if <60 and not immune, zoster (>50), Tdap, pneumococcal, RSV.
:::

## Choosing non-insulin drugs
![Oral anti-diabetic therapy algorithm](assets/figure-0174-00.webp)
{Diab Care 2021;44:S111} Use **dual therapy when A1c is >1.5% above goal.**
| Class | Drugs and doses | A1c drop | Best for | Avoid / cautions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metformin** (less hepatic glucose output) | 500–1000 mg BID | 1–2% | **First line**; slight weight loss, better lipids | Don't start if GFR <45; stop if <30; metabolic acidosis. GI upset, B12 deficiency, lactic acidosis with severe liver/kidney disease or hypoperfusion |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** (glucose lost in urine) | Empagliflozin 10–25 mg, dapagliflozin 5–10 mg, canagliflozin 100–300 mg daily | 0.8–0.9% | Fewer CV events and deaths, fewer HF admissions, slower CKD {NEJM 2019;380:2295}; weight loss; BP −3–5; low hypoglycemia risk | Start if eGFR ≥20; stop if <20; prior DKA. GU infections, euglycemic DKA (**hold 3–4 days before surgery** until eating), dehydration, possible LDL rise; canagliflozin: fractures, ?amputation |
| **GLP-1 RAs** (glucose-dependent insulin release, satiety) | Semaglutide 0.25–2.4 mg weekly, dulaglutide 0.75–4.5 mg weekly, liraglutide 0.6–1.8 mg daily; **tirzepatide** (GLP-1/GIP) 2.5–15 mg weekly | 0.5–2%; tirzepatide 2.6% | Fewer CV events; weight loss; **first injectable, before basal insulin**; low hypoglycemia risk | History of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2 (rodent black box); avoid exenatide if GFR 30–45. Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, slow gastric emptying, pancreatitis |
| **DPP-4 inhibitors** | Sitagliptin 25–100 mg, linagliptin 5 mg daily | 0.5–0.8% | Weight neutral; low hypoglycemia; linagliptin in CKD/ESRD | Weak. Saxagliptin and alogliptin: more HF admissions; joint pain |
| **Sulfonylureas** | Glipizide 2.5–20 mg, glimepiride 1–8 mg daily | 1–2% | Cheap | Type 1, DKA. Weight gain, hypoglycemia (especially glyburide), possible CV mortality. Low cross-reactivity with sulfa allergy |
| **Meglitinides** | Repaglinide 0.5–4 mg before meals | 0.5–0.7% | Like short-acting bolus insulin; CKD; less nocturnal hypoglycemia | Severe liver disease, gemfibrozil. Weight gain, hypoglycemia, TID dosing, higher levels with clopidogrel |
| **Pioglitazone** (insulin sensitizer) | 15–30 mg daily | 1–1.6% | Low hypoglycemia; may help NASH | NYHA III/IV HF (black box: fluid retention), bladder cancer history, kidney impairment. Weight gain, fractures |

:::tip Prescribing pearls
- **Metformin:** GI symptoms fade. Titrate slowly (250–500 mg/week), take with food, or use XR. Benefits are dose-dependent, so keep the highest tolerated dose.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors:** explain the diuretic effect and to drink fluids (lowers euglycemic DKA risk). Low dose for a month, then titrate. Potency: empagliflozin > canagliflozin > dapagliflozin. HF benefit doesn't depend on A1c lowering.
- **GLP-1 RAs:** start low to limit GI effects; increase monthly. Weight loss: semaglutide > dulaglutide > liraglutide.
:::

## Insulin {Diab Care 2024;47:S158; Diabetes Care 2025;48:S181–S206}
### Basal
- **Start if:** A1c ≥9%, random glucose ≥300, fasting ≥250, or symptomatic; suspected type 1 or LADA; on two agents with A1c >8% (age <65) or >8.5% (≥65) twice >3 months apart; or A1c rising fast. Patient must be able to check glucose; consider a diabetes educator.
- **Dose:** A1c <8% → 0.1–0.2 U/kg/day. A1c >8% → 0.2–0.3 U/kg/day.
- **Agent:** long-acting (glargine, detemir or degludec daily) over NPH BID (less hypoglycemia). Pens are easier but cost more than vial and syringe.
- **Titrate:** +2–4 U or 10–15% every 2–3 days until fasting glucose is 80–130 without lows. Engaged patients can self-titrate.
- **Low** (hypoglycemia or fasting <80 without a clear reason): cut by 10–20% or 4 U, whichever is more.
- **Need more control?** Add a GLP-1 RA or tirzepatide **before** mealtime insulin (less hypoglycemia and weight gain).

### Mealtime (prandial)
- **Start if** A1c is above goal on basal >0.5 U/kg/day with fasting glucose already 80–130.
- **Option 1:** rapid-acting insulin before the largest meal: 4 U, or 0.1 U/kg, or 10% of the basal dose.
- **Option 2:** premixed insulin (e.g. 70/30, or NPH + regular) BID before breakfast and dinner. Split the current basal dose ⅔ AM / ⅓ PM or ½ / ½. Only if the patient eats 3 regular meals.
- **Titrate:** +1–2 U or 10–15% every 3 days to pre-meal 80–130 and 1–2 h post-meal <180. Still above goal → add rapid-acting insulin to another meal.
- **Low:** cut by 10–20% or 4 U, whichever is more.

### Supplies
- **Pen needles** for pens; otherwise needle + syringe. 32 G 4 mm hurts least; larger patients and big doses may need longer or thicker needles.
- **Syringes** come in boxes of 100. Pick the smallest barrel that holds the dose (clearer markings):
| Barrel | Dose |
|---|---|
| 3/10 mL | ≤30 U |
| 1/2 mL | 31–50 U |
| 1 mL | 51–100 U |
- **Alcohol swabs** (or soap and water).
- **Glucometer and strips:** strips are brand-specific, 50–100 per box; some meters read results aloud. Check which brand the patient's insurance covers.
- **Durable medical equipment (strips, meters) needs an ICD-10 code on the prescription.**

## Pregnancy {Diabetes Care 2024;47:S5-S10}
- **Tighter targets:** A1c <6.0–6.5% and fasting glucose <95.
- **Insulin is preferred** (requirements may rise). Stop GLP-1 RAs. Metformin and glyburide aren't first line (efficacy and safety concerns).
- Don't aim for weight loss (risk of small-for-gestational-age babies). Encourage breastfeeding.
