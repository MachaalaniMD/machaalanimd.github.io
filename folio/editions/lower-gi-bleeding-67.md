# Lower GI Bleeding
> Bleeding below the ligament of Treitz, usually diverticular and self-limited. Stable → colonoscopy within 24–36 hours. Unstable → exclude an upper source with EGD, then CTA and IR. Transfuse to Hgb >7. {ACG: AJG 2023;118:208}

## First steps
- **Presentation:** hematochezia (maroon or bright red blood, clots). Melena is rare (needs ~14 h in the gut). BUN/Cr is usually <20 with normal kidney function. Brisk upper bleeding can also cause bright red blood.
- **Labs:** CBC q2–12h (by severity), CMP, coags, type and screen
- **Access and blood:** ≥2 IVs 18 G or larger; crystalloid for hypotension; transfuse to Hgb >7 (>8 with CAD) {NEJM 2013;368:11; Lancet 2017;2:354; Annals 2019;171:805; JAMA 2021;325:552}, without delay if actively bleeding. Hct lags 24–72 h. After ~4 units, activate massive transfusion (see *Transfusion Medicine*). Platelets >30K (>50K for procedures).
- **Risk scores:** ABC predicts 30-day mortality; **Oakland <8** may predict safe discharge. {JAMA 2020;3:e209630}

:::warn Hemodynamically unstable? Think upper GI
10–15% of severe hematochezia is an upper GI bleed. {AJG 2010;105:2636} Shock index (HR/SBP) >1 = significant bleed.
:::

## Find and stop it
::: Unstable | Shock index >1, ongoing bleeding
1. **EGD** to exclude an upper source (GI)
2. **CTA**, then **IR angiography** with embolization if a source is seen (risk of bowel ischemia, vascular injury)
3. **Surgery** (subtotal colectomy) if it can't be located or controlled
:::
::: Stable | Bleeding slowed or stopped
- **Colonoscopy** for most hospitalized patients, unless bleeding stopped and a colonoscopy within the last year showed diverticulosis
- **Timing:** 24–36 h is safe for most stable patients {Gastro 2020;158:1}. Prep: no solids or red liquids for 8 h before.
- **CTA** has low yield for minor or resolved bleeding. Tagged RBC scans are more sensitive but localize poorly (rarely used).
:::

### Anticoagulants and antiplatelets
Mostly extrapolated from upper GI bleeding (see *Upper GI Bleeding*). **Aspirin for primary prevention:** stop and generally don't restart. **Aspirin for secondary prevention:** don't hold; if held, restart soon after bleeding stops. {Gastro 2016;151:271}

## Causes {NEJM 2017;376:1054}
| Clue | Likely cause (share of LGIB) |
|---|---|
| **Painless** | Diverticular (30–65%), angioectasia (5–10%), hemorrhoids (5–20%) |
| **Abdominal pain** | Ischemic colitis (5–20%), IBD (3–5%), perforation |
| **Weight loss** | Cancer or polyp (2–15%), IBD |
| **Fever, diarrhea** | Infectious colitis (2–5%), IBD, acute mesenteric ischemia |
| **AS, ESRD or LVAD** | Angioectasia; Heyde syndrome |
| **Recent colonoscopy** | Post-polypectomy (2–7%) |
| **Constipation** | Stercoral ulcer (0–5%) |
| **Pelvic radiation** | Radiation proctitis/colitis (0–2%) |
| **NSAIDs** | NSAID colopathy (0–2%) |
| **Liver disease** | Colorectal varices (0–3%) |
| **AF** | Acute mesenteric ischemia |
| **Prior GI surgery** | Anastomotic ulcer |
| **AAA repair** | Aortoenteric fistula |
| **Recent stem cell transplant / DLI** | GVHD |
| **Unstable** | Brisk upper GI bleed (13%) |

## No source after EGD and colonoscopy {ACG: AJG 2015;110:1265}
~75% are in the small bowel; ~25% were missed upper or lower sources.
- **Small bowel causes:** angioectasia, IBD (especially <40), Dieulafoy, tumor, Meckel diverticulum (<40), polyposis (<40), NSAID ulcers. Rare: varices, portal hypertensive enteropathy, amyloid, HHT, Kaposi, connective tissue disease, congenital vascular lesions. **Heyde syndrome:** aortic stenosis + GI bleeding + acquired von Willebrand disease.
- **Work-up** {ASGE 2017;85:22}:
  1. **Capsule endoscopy** first (not with known strictures; erythromycin 250 mg IV after placement helps passage)
  2. **Second-look EGD ± push enteroscopy** (reaches the proximal 60 cm of jejunum) if upper symptoms recur; **repeat colonoscopy** if hematochezia recurs
  3. **CT enterography** if capsule is negative or stricture risk (IBD, radiation, prior surgery); CTE > MRE
  4. **Balloon enteroscopy** to treat a suspected or capsule-found lesion
  5. **Brisk bleeding:** CTA then angiography if stable; embolization, enteroscopy or surgery if unstable
- **No source:** replete iron; consider octreotide or anti-angiogenic therapy; AVR for Heyde with ongoing bleeding.
