# Sodium Disorders
> Sodium problems are water problems. For hyponatremia: confirm low tonicity, ask whether ADH is acting (urine osm), then use volume status and urine Na. Correct by 4–6 mEq/L a day and never more than 8. {JAMA 2022;328(3):280-291}

## Hyponatremia: recognize and work up
**Symptoms:** often none; AMS, headache, nausea/vomiting, weakness, seizures, signs of raised ICP, salt craving.

**Work up when Na <130.** Ask about water and diet intake, urination, new meds, chronicity, endocrine symptoms, volume status, and surgery with irrigation (prostate, bladder, uterine).

**Send before treating:**
- **Serum:** BMP, osmolality; consider uric acid, TSH, AM cortisol
- **Urine:** UA, osmolality, Na, uric acid

:::key Three questions
1. **Is tonicity low?** Serum osm can mislead when ineffective osmoles (BUN, ethanol) are present.
2. **Is ADH acting?** Urine osm >100 means yes. Estimate it from the UA: last 2 digits of specific gravity × 30 (SG 1.010 ≈ 300).
3. **Is the kidney holding Na?** Urine Na <30 means RAAS is active (low effective volume); >30 means it isn't.
:::

**Extra clues** {Eur J Inter Med 2016;29:22; JCEM 2008;93:2991; Ann Intern Med 1980;93:716; Cureus 2020;12:e7762}
- **SIADH:** serum uric acid often <4, BUN <5, FEUA ≥10–12% (small studies: 100% PPV for SIADH or thiazide hyponatremia; FEUA ≤8% = 100% NPV)
- **Low effective volume:** uric acid often >5, BUN high, FEUA <4%
- **NS trial:** in hypovolemia, NS raises Na. In SIADH it raises Na briefly, then lowers it (Na is excreted, water kept).

## Hyponatremia: causes and treatment
| | ADH absent (urine osm <100) | Urine Na <30: kidney holding Na | Urine Na >30: kidney wasting Na |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What's happening** | Intake overwhelms the kidney's ability to dilute | Low effective volume; ADH appropriate | Kidney won't dilute; ADH inappropriate |
| **Causes** | Primary polydipsia (psychogenic, endurance athletes, MDMA); low solute intake (tea and toast, beer potomania) | True hypovolemia; third spacing (pancreatitis, muscle injury); heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome | SIADH (CNS disease, pain, nausea, lung infection or cancer, meds {NEJM 2023;389:1499-1509}, idiopathic); thiazides, ACEi/ARBs, ESRD, cerebral salt wasting, adrenal insufficiency, severe hypothyroidism |
| **Treat** | Polydipsia: restrict fluid. Low solute: reintroduce solute **slowly**; these correct fast and overcorrect easily | Hypovolemia: give volume (high overcorrection risk: watch urine output, may need DDAVP). Pancreatitis: volume. Heart failure, nephrosis: diurese. Cirrhosis: treat only if Na <120; fluid restrict, correct K; vaptans or 3% only if severe or awaiting transplant | Treat the cause. SIADH: see below |

- The urine osm cutoff rises as GFR falls: with GFR <15 the kidney can't dilute below ~200–250.

### SIADH
- **Mild:** fluid restrict to 1–1.5 L/day
- Add **NaCl tabs** (1 g TID), **urea** (15–60 g/day {KI 2015;87:268}) or an **SGLT2 inhibitor** {JASN 2023;34(2)322}
- If urine osm >2× serum osm, or urine Na + K > serum Na: NaCl tabs + **furosemide** 10–20 mg BID, or a **vaptan** with renal input {NEJM 2006;355:2099}

## Hyponatremia: how fast to correct
Speed depends on symptoms, severity and cause. {JASN 2017;28:1340} **Call renal** if the patient needs 3% saline or a DDAVP clamp.
| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| **Severe symptoms** (seizure, AMS) | 3% NaCl 100 mL over 10 min; repeat up to 3× until symptoms resolve or Na rises 5 (max 8–10 in 24 h) |
| **Severe, no symptoms** (Na <120) | 3% NaCl at the Na correction rate, usually 15–30 mL/h, until Na ≥125. Na <120 carries 6–10% in-hospital mortality {Clin J Am Soc Nephrol 2018;13:641}. Consider a proactive DDAVP clamp if ODS risk is high |
| **Mild–moderate** (Na 130–134 or 120–129) | Treat the cause (fluids, fluid restriction, diuresis, or stopping diuretics). Overly slow correction may raise mortality {JAMA Intern Med 2025;185(1):38-51} |

- **Goal:** raise Na 4–6 mEq/L per 24 h toward ≥125; a 4–6 rise usually relieves symptoms.
- **Overcorrection:** a rise >8 in 24 h or >18 in 48 h. With high ODS risk, some guidelines count >4–6 in 24 h.

:::warn Osmotic demyelination (ODS)
- **Onset 2–6 days later:** dysarthria, dysphagia, paresis, behavior change or AMS, locked-in syndrome. MRI shows it ~4 weeks after symptoms.
- **Risk:** starting Na ≤110 (unlikely if >120 {Hosp Pract 1995;37:128}), malnutrition, alcohol use, cirrhosis, low K
- **Fast correction is likeliest** in hypovolemic and low-solute hyponatremia. {CJASN 2018;13:984} Watch for a water diuresis after fluids (urine output >100 mL/h).
- **Potassium counts:** 1 mEq of K raises Na like 1 mEq of Na, so K repletion can overcorrect.
:::

### DDAVP clamp and relowering {Am J Kidney Dis 2022;79(6):890}
- **If Na is heading too fast:** DDAVP 2–4 mcg IV/SC q6–8h, Na q4–6h, until Na ≥125; add 3% at the correction rate if needed
- **If already overcorrected,** bring Na back within the goal (rise <8/24 h, or <4–6 if high ODS risk) urgently:
  - D5W 6 mL/kg ideal body weight over 2 h (lowers Na ~2), checking Na q2h until at goal, **plus**
  - DDAVP 2–4 mcg IV/SC q6h for 24–48 h, even after the goal is reached

## Hypernatremia
> Water loss in excess of salt loss. {Crit Care 2013;17:206; NEJM 2015;372:55}

**Causes**
- No access to water or impaired thirst; diabetes insipidus
- **Renal losses** (urine osm <800): less ADH or less kidney response. Post-ATN, osmotic diuresis, DI, loop diuretics, hypercalcemia, older age.
- **Extrarenal losses** (urine osm >800): NG output, vomiting, diarrhea, insensible losses, hypodipsia

**Work-up:** water and diet history, urine output, meds (especially lithium), recent brain surgery, malignancy. Labs: BMP, serum and urine osm. Urine osm <300 suggests AVP deficiency or resistance (DI).

### Treatment
BMP q12–24h and strict I/O. Lower Na by about **12 mEq/L per 24 h**. Accidental overcorrection is rarely dangerous in adults. {CJASN 2019;14:656}
1. **Free water deficit (L)** ≈ (current Na − goal Na) / 3, using the 24 h goal Na
2. **Add ongoing losses:**
   - Insensible: usually 700–900 mL/day, more with burns, fever, diarrhea
   - Urine free water: 24 h urine output × (1 − (urine Na + urine K) / serum Na). Shortcut: 0 × the 1st L + 0.5 × the 2nd and 3rd L + 1 × anything beyond 3 L
3. **Free water today** = deficit + ongoing losses, by mouth, tube-feed flushes or IV D5W. For DI, see *Pituitary Disorders* for DDAVP.
