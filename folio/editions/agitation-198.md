# Agitation
> Safety first, restraints last. Notice escalation early, fix simple causes (pain, hunger, retention), de-escalate verbally, and offer oral medication early, matched to the underlying cause.

## Goals
1. Safety of the patient and staff
2. Help the patient manage distress
3. Avoid restraints
4. Avoid coercion

## Early steps
- **Watch for escalation:** anger or fear, yelling, pacing, sweating, aggressive body language. Listen to nurses and PCAs.
- **Fix quick causes:** hunger, thirst, pain, communication breakdown, urinary retention, constipation. Work up delirium, psychiatric illness, intoxication or withdrawal.
- **Offer oral medication early,** and consider standing doses if it keeps recurring.
- **If restraints are needed,** always give medication to shorten the time in them; reassess often and remove ASAP. Restrained patients risk aspiration, rhabdo, injury and delirium.

:::warn
Restraint and seclusion can traumatize, carry real morbidity and mortality, and represent a failure of earlier steps.
:::

## Verbal de-escalation: DEFUSE {West J Emerg Med 2012;13:1}
- **D**ecide if it's appropriate: the patient is responsive, engageable and not an immediate threat. Have a team, but only one person talks.
- **E**nsure safety: backup ready; remove loose objects; stay two arm's lengths away; hands visible; stand at an angle.
- **F**orm a relationship: give your name and role, ask what they like to be called, ask "Will you let us help?" Short sentences, simple words, time to process.
- **U**se their interests: find out what they want and feel. Agree where you can (in truth, in principle, or agree to disagree). Reassure them you won't let harm come to them.
- **S**et limits: state consequences matter-of-factly; offer choices (even oral vs IV medication); repeat until heard.
- **E**nforce and evaluate: if violence seems imminent, step back and get help. Afterward, debrief staff and the patient.

## Medications
:::key Match the drug to the cause
- Alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal, catatonia, stimulant intoxication → **benzodiazepines**
- Psychosis → **antipsychotics**
- Mania → antipsychotics, mood stabilizers, benzodiazepines
- **No antipsychotics** for catatonia or NMS
:::
**Before choosing, check:** standing meds that could be used; allergies; heart disease, arrhythmia, OSA, COPD, Parkinson's, fall risk; what has already been given today (near the daily max?); QTc. Don't delay medication to get an ECG; get one when you can.

**Always offer an oral option first** if the patient can safely take it. Consider psychiatry if IM medication is needed.
| Drug | Routes | Dose | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Olanzapine** | PO, ODT, IM, IV | 2.5–10 mg; max 20 mg/day | **No parenteral benzodiazepine within 4 h of parenteral olanzapine** (fatal respiratory depression) |
| **Haloperidol** | PO, IM, IV | 2.5–10 mg; max 30 mg/day | IM: highest EPS risk. IV: most QTc prolongation (check if QTc >550), especially in the medically ill {Psychosomatics 2018;59:105} |
| **Quetiapine** | PO | 12.5–100 mg | Orthostatic hypotension |
| **Trazodone** | PO | 12.5–100 mg | Good in older adults (low delirium risk); can trigger mania in bipolar disorder |
| **Risperidone** | PO, ODT | 0.5–2 mg; max 8 mg/day | Most EPS of the second-generation drugs |
| **Chlorpromazine** | PO, IM, IV | 25–100 mg | Hypotension; lowers seizure threshold; avoid in pregnancy |
| **Lorazepam** | PO, IM, IV | 0.5–2 mg | Delirium in older adults; hypercarbia with COPD/OSA. Same 4 h olanzapine warning |
| **Diphenhydramine** | PO, IM, IV | 12.5–50 mg | Sedating; usually an adjunct to IM haloperidol to prevent EPS. "5-2-50" (haloperidol 5, lorazepam 2, diphenhydramine 50) only with caution for very severe aggression |
| **Hydroxyzine** | PO | 25–50 mg | Fast anxiolytic; delirium risk; possible QTc prolongation |

### Special populations
- **Older adults:** PO trazodone 12.5–25 mg or quetiapine 12.5–25 mg. Can't take PO → olanzapine 2.5–5 mg IM/IV.
- **Under 18:** clonidine 0.05 mg PO (<35 kg) or 0.1 mg (>35 kg). Severe and can't take PO → olanzapine IM 1.25 mg (<25 kg), 2.5 mg (25–70 kg), 5 mg (>70 kg).
- **Parkinson's / Lewy body dementia:** PO quetiapine 12.5–50 mg; otherwise lorazepam 0.5–1 mg IV/IM. Avoid first-generation antipsychotics, olanzapine and risperidone (dopamine blockade worsens symptoms).
- **Withdrawal:** see *Alcohol Use Disorder & Withdrawal* and *Opioid Use Disorder & Withdrawal*.

**Call psychiatry** when agitation is severe and refractory to medication and non-drug measures, when specialized treatment (e.g. ECT) may be needed, or when escalating PRNs risk NMS or QT prolongation.
