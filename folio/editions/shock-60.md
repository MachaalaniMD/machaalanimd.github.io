# Shock
> Tissue hypoxia from too little oxygen delivery or extraction, causing organ damage. Decide which of four types it is (often from the exam and a bedside echo), treat the cause, and support MAP >65. {NEJM 2013;369:1726}

## Recognize it
- **Hypotension:** SBP <90, or a fall >40 from baseline
- **Organ dysfunction:** urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h, altered mentation, metabolic acidosis (± anion gap, high lactate, low bicarbonate from kidney failure)
- **Skin:** cool and clammy (low output) vs warm and flushed (distributive)
- Any of these, **including BP**, can be normal in shock. Keep a high index of suspicion.

**Initial workup:** focused H&P, secure access, review meds, ECG, CXR, ABG/VBG, CBC with diff, CMP, troponin, lactate, central venous O2 saturation, urine output.

:::note What sets blood pressure
- **MAP = cardiac output × SVR**; MAP ≈ ⅔ DBP + ⅓ SBP
- **Cardiac output = HR × stroke volume**; stroke volume depends on preload, afterload and contractility
- **SVR** depends on vessel diameter and length, and blood viscosity
:::

## Which type?
| | Distributive (66%) | Hypovolemic (16%) | Cardiogenic (16%) | Obstructive (2%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Low SVR, impaired O2 extraction | Low output, poor O2 delivery | Low output, poor O2 delivery | Low output, poor O2 delivery |
| Examples | Sepsis, anaphylaxis, adrenal insufficiency, liver failure, drugs/toxins, neurogenic | Hemorrhage, GI losses, third spacing (pancreatitis) | MI, heart failure, myocarditis, severe valve disease, arrhythmia | PE, tension pneumothorax, tamponade |
| Extremities | Warm, dry | Cold, dry | Cold, wet | Cold, dry |
| CVP / PCWP | Low or normal | Very low (<5) | Very high (>7) | High or normal |
| CO / ScvO2 | High or normal (may be low before fluids) | Low (<70%) | Low (<70%) | Low (<70%) |
| SVR | Very low | High | High | High |
| Echo | Normal chambers, normal/high function | Small chambers, normal/high function | Large chambers, poor function | Effusion (tamponade) or dilated RV (PE, PTX) |

{NEJM 2013;369:840; NEJM 2001;345:588; NEJM 2018;378:370}

:::tip Quick bedside read
- **High output:** wide pulse pressure, low diastolic BP, warm extremities, normal capillary refill
- **Low output:** narrow pulse pressure, cold extremities, slow capillary refill
:::

## Treat by type
::: Distributive | Warm, vasodilated
- **Septic:** see *Sepsis*. **Anaphylactic:** see *Anaphylaxis*.
- **Neurogenic:** suspect with TBI or spinal cord injury. Resuscitate with **NS, not LR** (LR is slightly hypotonic and worsens cerebral edema); watch for raised ICP.
:::
::: Hypovolemic | Cold, dry, empty
- **Access:** 3 large IVs (16 G or bigger) if possible; consider a Cordis. Triple-lumen catheters and PICCs infuse too slowly.
- **Causes:** poor intake, GI losses (vomiting, stool, fistula), GU losses (osmotic diuresis, diuretics, hypoaldosteronism, salt wasting), burns, heat stroke, third spacing (pancreatitis, low albumin, trauma), hemorrhage (trauma, surgery, GI bleed)
- **Fluids:** crystalloid boluses (LR over NS), except in hemorrhage, where you give only what bridges you to blood
- **Major bleeding:**
  - STAT coags, CBC, fibrinogen, ionized calcium; hold pressure; secure access
  - Reverse anticoagulants. INR >2.5 → 4-factor PCC (no INR goal in ESLD); platelets <50 → platelets; fibrinogen <100 → cryo; iCa <1.10 → IV calcium
  - Variceal bleed suspected → octreotide (see *Upper GI Bleeding*)
  - Surgical, trauma or gynecologic bleeding → TXA 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 h; or aminocaproic acid 5 g IV over 1 h, then 1 g/h for 8 h
  - Transfuse to hemodynamics; call emergency release blood or massive transfusion (see *MTP*)
  - **Source control:** surgery, vascular, GI or IR depending on the site; STAT CTA if stable
:::
::: Cardiogenic | Cold, wet, congested
- **Pump failure:** dobutamine 2–5 mcg/kg/min (unclear role in mixed septic shock) and diuresis. In MI, revascularize (PCI or CABG).
- **Arrhythmia:** tachycardia → synchronized cardioversion, vagal maneuvers, adenosine, β-blocker. Bradycardia → atropine, pacing.
- **Mechanical** (e.g. acute valve regurgitation): hemodynamic support plus endovascular or surgical repair.
:::
::: Obstructive | Cold, high CVP
- **Relieve the obstruction** (see *Pericardial Disease*, *VTE Management*)
- **Tension pneumothorax:** O2, needle decompression or chest tube; avoid positive-pressure ventilation and BiPAP
:::

## Support for any type
- **Vasopressors:** titrate to MAP >65 (cardiogenic: MAP >60). Acidosis blunts the vascular response to pressors. See *Vasopressors*.
- **Intubation** if needed (respiratory failure, unable to compensate for acidosis, marked instability), with pressors ready. Pulse oximetry may be unreliable with vasoconstriction; use an ABG.
- **PA catheter:** no benefit in undifferentiated shock or ICU patients overall; may improve mortality in cardiogenic shock. {Cochrane Rev 2013; JAMA 2005;294:1625; Lancet 2005;366:472; JACC 2020;903-913}
- **Steroids:** known adrenal insufficiency or chronic steroid use → stress dose (hydrocortisone 50 mg q6h or 100 mg q8h for 5–7 days)
- **Bicarbonate:** pH <7.1, or <7.2 with severe AKI (amps or drip). Mortality benefit only in AKI. {Lancet 2018;392:31}

:::warn Refractory shock: think again
Dynamic LVOT obstruction (from inotropes), anaphylaxis, abdominal compartment syndrome, abdominal catastrophe (steroids can mask it), new obstruction (HOCM, PE), hypocalcemia, refractory vasodilation.
**Rescue options:** hydroxocobalamin, angiotensin II, methylene blue, thiamine, ECMO.
:::
