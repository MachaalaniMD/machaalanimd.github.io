# Upper GI Bleeding
> Bleeding above the ligament of Treitz. Stabilize first (two large IVs, transfuse to Hgb >7), give IV PPI, add octreotide and ceftriaxone if cirrhotic, and get an EGD within 24 hours.

## First steps
:::key Stabilize, then intervene
1. **Access and airway:** NPO, at least 2 IVs 18 G or larger. Intubate if aspiration risk is high (large hematemesis, AMS).
2. **Labs:** CBC q2–8h, CMP, coags, type and screen, orthostatics, abdominal and rectal exam (smear stool on a white napkin to judge the color)
3. **Volume:** isotonic crystalloid for hypotension; **transfuse to Hgb >7** (>8 with CAD). Don't delay blood if bleeding is active. {NEJM 2013;368:11; Lancet 2017;2:354; Annals 2019;171:805}
4. **Correct coagulopathy:** platelets to >50K; INR >1.5 → consider PCC (less volume and faster than FFP); uremia → consider DDAVP. **TXA doesn't help.**
5. **Meds before EGD:** IV PPI; erythromycin; octreotide + ceftriaxone if cirrhotic
6. **Call GI** for EGD; surgery/IR if unstable or endoscopy isn't an option
:::
- Hct lags the bleed by 24–72 h.
- **Massive bleeding** (usually after ~4 units RBC): activate the massive transfusion protocol (see *Transfusion Medicine*).
- **Don't over-transfuse:** extra volume raises portal pressure and worsens variceal bleeding.
- **Cirrhosis:** INR doesn't reflect bleeding risk; avoid FFP (volume raises portal pressure).
- **EGD timing:** within 24 h. 0–6 h is no better than 6–24 h for non-variceal bleeds. {NEJM 2020;382:1299}
- **Avoid:** barium studies (they block EGD and angiography); routine NG lavage (no benefit). {ERGH 2017;12:63}

## How sick is the patient?
- **High-risk features:** hypotension, tachycardia, INR >1.5, Hgb <10, AMS, syncope, age >65, liver disease, heart failure
- **Scores:** Glasgow-Blatchford and ABC. **Blatchford 0–1** = low rebleeding risk; consider outpatient care. {Annals 2019;171:805; BMJ 2017;356:6432; Gut 2021;70:707}
- **ICU** if: BP <90 with HR >100 on two checks 30 min apart; Hct <20 / Hgb <7 with significant active bleeding in the last 12 h; or needing >2 L fluid or 2 units RBC to stay stable or keep Hct >25

**Symptoms:** hematemesis, melena, or hematochezia if brisk. Clots in the stool make an upper source less likely.

## Medications
| When | Drug | Dose / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Before EGD** | **Pantoprazole IV** | 40 mg BID. Fewer high-risk lesions needing therapy; clinical effect before EGD unclear {Cochrane Rev 2010} |
| | **Erythromycin IV** | 250 mg 30 min before EGD to clear the stomach {Ann Gastroenterol 2016;29:312}. Order STAT (pharmacy takes time); metoclopramide 10 mg IV if unavailable |
| **Cirrhosis** | **Octreotide IV** | 50 mcg bolus (may repeat in the first hour if still bleeding), then 50 mcg/h for 2–5 days |
| | **Ceftriaxone IV** | 1 g q24h × 7 days: prevents infection and lowers mortality {Gastro 2006;131:1049; APT 2011;34:509} |
| | **β-blockers** | Hold until day 5, then restart |
| **After EGD: high-risk ulcer** | **Pantoprazole IV** | 40 mg BID × 72 h (fewer rebleeds and repeat EGDs), then oral PPI BID for 2–8 weeks. Treat H. pylori if positive |
| **Angiodysplasia** | Long-term octreotide or bevacizumab | With GI input {APT 2012;36:587} |

Read the GI procedure note for diet, PPI and follow-up instructions.

**Rebleeding:** repeat EGD, or CTA plus IR/surgery. For varices, consider balloon tamponade or TIPS.

## Anticoagulants and antiplatelets
{Gastrointest Endosc 2016;83:3; Am J Gastroenterol 2022;117:542}
| Drug | During the bleed | Restart |
|---|---|---|
| **Warfarin** | Hold. Reverse with PCC if needed; FFP and vitamin K **not** recommended | After hemostasis (UFH bridge ~48 h if indicated). Restarting within 7 days lowers thrombosis and death in AF {Am J Cardiol 2014;113:662} |
| **DOAC** | Hold. Reversal (idarucizumab, andexanet alfa, PCC) **not** recommended | Within 72 h if thrombotic risk is high; within 7 days if low |
| **Aspirin** | Continue if bleed risk low–moderate; hold if high (unless recent PCI/ACS) | Once hemostasis is confirmed; not restarting raises 30-day mortality {Annals 2010;152:1}. Add a PPI if ulcer |
| **DAPT** after PCI/ACS | Talk to cardiology. Very recent (<30 days PCI, <90 days ACS): continue both unless life-threatening. Older: continue aspirin, holding the P2Y12 inhibitor is lower risk | Within 1–5 days, depending on the course |

Restarting sooner lowers vascular events but raises rebleeding. {APT 2019;50:8}

## Causes {Dig Dis Sci 2018;63:1286}
| Cause | Share | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Peptic ulcer | ~50% | Duodenal > gastric. H. pylori, NSAIDs, alcohol, tobacco, Cameron lesion, Zollinger-Ellison |
| Esophagitis / gastritis | ~30% | GERD, pills (doxycycline, iron), aspirin, NSAIDs, clopidogrel, alcohol, infection |
| Varices | ~5% | Esophageal > gastric |
| Vascular | ~5–10% | Dieulafoy, AVM, GAVE, HHT, radiation ectasia, aortoenteric fistula |
| Trauma | ~5% | Mallory-Weiss, foreign body, Boerhaave |
| Tumor | ~5% | Primary > metastatic |
| After procedures | Varies | Polypectomy, sphincterotomy |
| Biliary / pancreatic | Rare | Hemobilia, hemosuccus pancreaticus |
