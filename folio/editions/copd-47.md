# COPD
> Persistent symptoms with fixed obstruction (post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.7). Treat by GOLD group (A, B, E). For exacerbations: SpO2 88–92%, nebulizers, prednisone 40 mg × 5 days, antibiotics when sputum turns purulent, and early NIPPV for hypercapnic acidosis. {GOLD 2025}

## Exacerbation {GOLD 2025; ERJ 2017;49}
- **History:** worse dyspnea, sputum or cough over <14 days; prior exacerbations, steroids, intubations, antibiotics. **Differential:** viral infection/pneumonia, heart failure, VTE.
- **Work-up:** CXR, VBG/ABG, ECG, troponin, NT-proBNP, sputum culture, RVP, CRP. Consider PE: found in 25% of severe exacerbations without a clear trigger. {Annals 2006;144:390}

:::key Treat
- **Oxygen to SpO2 88–92%.** More O2 worsens hypercapnia (Haldane effect, loss of hypoxic vasoconstriction) and raises mortality. {BMJ 2010;341:c5462}
- **Bronchodilators:** stacked DuoNebs (×3 in 1 h), then standing DuoNebs q4h with albuterol q2h PRN, spacing out as able
- **Steroids:** prednisone 40 mg × 5 days. Oral = IV {Chest 2007;132:1741}; 5 days = 14 days {REDUCE, JAMA 2013;309:2223; Cochrane Rev 2018}. Severe: methylprednisolone 60–125 mg IV q6–12h × 72 h, or budesonide nebs. Higher doses or longer courses raise pneumonia risk.
- **NIPPV** for respiratory acidosis (pH <7.35 and PaCO2 >45), severe dyspnea, high work of breathing or persistent hypoxemia: lowers mortality, intubation and length of stay. {Cochrane Rev 2004}
- **Intubate** for NIPPV failure, arrest, aspiration/vomiting, hemodynamic instability.
- **Antivirals:** oseltamivir for influenza, even after 48–72 h. **VTE prophylaxis.**
:::

### Antibiotics
Lower mortality, but it's hard to know who benefits. {Chest 2008;133:756; Cochrane Rev 2012}
- **Give if:** all 3 cardinal symptoms (more dyspnea, more sputum, purulent sputum), or 2 including purulence, or needing NIPPV/ventilation
- CRP can help decide {NEJM 2019;381:111}; procalcitonin may help but was linked to higher mortality in the ICU. {Eur Resp Rev 2017;26; ICM 2018;44:428}
- **No Pseudomonas risk:** ceftriaxone, fluoroquinolone, amoxicillin-clavulanate, azithromycin or doxycycline. **Pseudomonas risk:** fluoroquinolone, cefepime or pip-tazo. Use prior sputum cultures.
- **Duration:** 5–7 days inpatient, 3–5 outpatient (varies by drug). Concurrent pneumonia → treat as *Community-Acquired Pneumonia*.

## Diagnose and stage {GOLD 2025; AJRCCM 2017;195:557}
1. **Diagnosis:** respiratory symptoms + FEV1/FVC <0.7 (or <LLN) after bronchodilator
2. **GOLD grade** (FEV1 % predicted): prognosis and non-drug care
3. **GOLD group** (exacerbations + symptoms): drug therapy
4. **Other tests:** lung volumes, DLCO (<60% tracks with symptoms, exercise limits, worse health), 6-minute walk, α1-antitrypsin

| Grade | FEV1 % predicted |
|---|---|
| 1 Mild | ≥80 |
| 2 Moderate | 50–79 |
| 3 Severe | 30–49 |
| 4 Very severe | <30 |

| | Few symptoms (mMRC 0–1, CAT <10) | More symptoms (mMRC ≥2, CAT ≥10) |
|---|---|---|
| **≥2 exacerbations or ≥1 admission** | **E** | **E** |
| **0–1 exacerbations, no admission** | **A** | **B** |

## Stable COPD {GOLD 2025; NEJM 2019;381:1257}
Goals: fewer symptoms and exacerbations, better health and survival.
| | GOLD A | GOLD B | GOLD E |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Start** | One long-acting bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA) | LABA + LAMA | LABA + LAMA; add ICS if eosinophils >300 or asthma features |
| **Rescue** | Short-acting bronchodilator | Short-acting bronchodilator | Short-acting bronchodilator |
| **Escalate** | Dyspnea or exacerbations → LABA + LAMA | **Dyspnea:** change device or molecule; nebulizers in severe COPD | **Exacerbations:** eosinophils >100 → LABA + LAMA + ICS {Lancet RM 2018;6:117}, consider a biologic (dupilumab, BOREAS). FEV1 <50% + chronic bronchitis + eosinophils <100 → roflumilast {Lancet 2015;385:857}. Former smokers → azithromycin {Lancet RM 2014;2:361; Cochrane Rev 2018} |
| **Notes** | Long-acting > short-acting unless dyspnea is occasional. Tiotropium may slow FEV1 decline in early COPD {NEJM 2017;377:923} | Dual > single therapy {Chest 2014;145:981; Cochrane Rev 2015} | Triple therapy cuts exacerbations but may raise pneumonia (TRIBUTE, IMPACT, ETHOS). Consider stopping ICS for pneumonia, but not if eosinophils >300 (exacerbations rebound) |

### Beyond inhalers
- **Smoking cessation** lowers mortality. {Annals 2005;142:233}
- **Vaccines:** influenza, COVID, PCV20, Tdap, zoster, RSV
- **Lung cancer screening:** yearly low-dose CT, age 50–80, ≥20 pack-years, smoked within 15 years {USPSTF 2021}
- **Pulmonary rehab** (groups B, E): better quality of life and exercise capacity {Cochrane Rev 2015}; possibly lower mortality {JAMA 2020;323:1813}
- **Home O2** if PaO2 ≤55 or SpO2 ≤88% (≤59 / ≤89% with pulmonary HTN, Hct >55% or heart failure); goal SpO2 >90%; reassess in 60–90 days
- **Nocturnal NIPPV** if daytime PaCO2 >53 with nocturnal SpO2 ≤88% despite 2 L O2, or persistent PaCO2 >53 after an exacerbation (fewer readmissions and deaths) {JAMA 2017;317:2177}
- **Procedures:** bronchoscopic lung volume reduction (emphysema with hyperinflation), bullectomy
- **Transplant referral:** progressive despite maximal therapy and not a surgical candidate, with BODE ≥5, frequent exacerbations, FEV1 20–25%, or rapidly rising BODE

## Differential
- **Pre-COPD:** symptoms with emphysema or abnormal physiology but no obstruction
- **PRISm:** symptoms with a normal ratio (>0.7) but FEV1 <80% after bronchodilator
- **Chronic bronchitis:** cough and sputum >3 months/year for 2+ years (overlaps COPD)
- **Asthma:** see *PFTs & Asthma*
- **Bronchiectasis:** lots of purulent sputum, bacterial infections, dilated bronchi on imaging
- **Obliterative bronchiolitis:** after lung or marrow transplant; air trapping on expiratory HRCT
- **Diffuse panbronchiolitis:** mostly Asian patients, chronic sinusitis, diffuse centrilobular nodules with hyperinflation
