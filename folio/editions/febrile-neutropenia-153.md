# Febrile Neutropenia
> Fever with ANC <500 is an emergency: cultures, then anti-pseudomonal β-lactam within 1 hour. Vancomycin only for specific indications; antifungals if fever persists 4–7 days. {J Oncol Pract 2019;15:19}

## Define
- **Fever:** T ≥100.4 °F sustained ≥1 h, or a single oral T >100.9
- **Neutropenia:** ANC <500, or <1000 and expected to fall below 500 within 48 h
- **Organisms:** a source is found in only ~40–50% (the rest are presumed gut translocation). When found: ~60% gram-positive (coag-negative staph > MSSA/MRSA, strep, enterococcus/VRE), especially with lines or mucositis; ~40% gram-negative (E. coli, Klebsiella > Pseudomonas). Fungi (Candida, molds) are more likely with prolonged neutropenia, broad antibiotics or TPN.

## Evaluate
- **History:** comorbidities, chemo type and timing, prior cultures, recent antibiotics or prophylaxis, meds, lines
- **Exam:** mouth (mucositis), skin, perineum (look; no digital rectal exam), line and port sites
- **Labs:** blood cultures from ≥2 sites (≥1 peripheral + each CVC lumen), UA/urine culture, CXR, sputum culture/Gram stain, respiratory viral panel, CMV PCR if post-HCT
- **Fungal markers:** LDH, β-D-glucan; galactomannan if Aspergillus risk is high (HCT, GVHD, neutropenia >10–14 days)
- **By symptoms:**
  - **GI:** CT A/P (neutropenic colitis/typhlitis); stool culture, O&P, C. diff if diarrhea
  - **Pulmonary:** CT chest ± bronchoscopy/BAL (especially if prolonged), sputum, MRSA nasal swab, RVP, Legionella antigen; endemic fungi if the CT and exposures fit
  - **Headache or sinus pain:** CT face/sinuses

### Risk {MASCC: JCO 2000;18:3038; CISNE: JCO 2015;33:465}
| High risk → admit | Low risk {JCO 2018;36:1443} |
|---|---|
| ANC ≤100 expected for ≥7 days; already inpatient when fever starts; comorbidities or infections (kidney/liver impairment, pneumonia, line infection); allogeneic HCT; grade 3–4 mucositis; alemtuzumab or CAR-T in the past 2 months | ANC ≤100 expected <7 days; no comorbidities; ECOG 0–1; good support at home → oral antibiotics after a brief stay, or outpatient |

## Empiric antibiotics {Clin Infect Dis 2011;52:e56; J Oncol Pract 2018;14:250}
:::warn Within 1 hour
Mortality rises with every hour of delay. {Antimicrob Agents Chemother 2014;58:3799}
:::
| Target | When | Regimen |
|---|---|---|
| **Gram-negatives incl. Pseudomonas** | **Always** | Cefepime 2 g q8h (or ceftazidime 2 g q8h), **or** pip-tazo 4.5 g q6h, **or** meropenem 1 g q8h |
| | ESBL risk | Meropenem 1 g q8h (2 g q8h for meningitis) |
| | Penicillin allergy | Verify the allergy; test-dose cefepime or meropenem via the penicillin hypersensitivity pathway; consider allergy consult. True allergy → aztreonam (not if ceftazidime-allergic: same side chain) + levofloxacin |
| | Low risk, oral (with attending) | Ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin + amoxicillin-clavulanate (or clindamycin if penicillin-allergic). Not if on fluoroquinolone prophylaxis |
| **Gram-positives** | **Not routine.** Add for: hypotension/severe sepsis, GPC bacteremia, line infection (rigors with infusion, site erythema), skin infection, radiographic pneumonia, severe mucositis with prior fluoroquinolone prophylaxis on ceftazidime | Vancomycin; VRE concern → daptomycin (not for lung infection) or linezolid |
| **Anaerobes** | Abdominal source, C. diff, oral ulcers/periodontal infection, post-obstructive pneumonia, necrotizing ulcers | Use a regimen with anaerobic coverage |
| **Fungi** | Fever >4–7 days despite antibiotics; positive fungal markers; suggestive CT chest (nodules, air crescent, cavity); positive BAL culture | Micafungin 100 mg q24h or amphotericin 5 mg/kg q24h (after 500 mL NS); ID consult if suspicion is high |

## Adjust and stop {Ther Adv Infect Dis 2022;9}
::: Fever resolves | Afebrile
- **Infection found:** narrow and finish the standard course; then consider fluoroquinolone prophylaxis until ANC >500
- **Cultures negative:** continue until afebrile **and** ANC >500, or narrow to fluoroquinolone prophylaxis after 4–5 afebrile days
:::
::: Fever persists 4–7 days | Still febrile
- **Stable:** don't broaden or add vancomycin. Consider engraftment, differentiation syndrome, GVHD, tumor lysis, drug fever, thrombophlebitis, hematoma, hepatosplenic candidiasis; talk to ID.
- **Worsening:** broaden ± antifungal; CT chest ± bronchoscopy for fungal infection
:::
::: Line infection | See Bloodstream Infections & Endocarditis
- **Coag-negative staph, non-VRE enterococcus:** can usually keep the line with IV antibiotics + antibiotic lock for 2 weeks
- **S. aureus, Pseudomonas, fungi:** remove the line. Other gram-negatives: discuss removal vs lock with ID
- **Complicated** (endocarditis, septic thrombosis, bacteremia/fungemia >72 h after removal): remove and treat 4–6 weeks
:::

## Prophylaxis
See NCCN for exact indications.
| Prophylaxis | Who |
|---|---|
| **Antibacterial** (fluoroquinolone) | High-risk (hematologic malignancy); intermediate risk at attending discretion |
| **Antifungal** (azole or echinocandin) | Select hematologic malignancies during neutropenia; early after HCT |
| **PJP** (TMP-SMX, atovaquone) | Prednisone ≥20 mg/day for ≥1 month, purine analogs, CD4 <200, after HCT or CAR-T |
| **HSV/VZV** (acyclovir, famciclovir) | Hematologic malignancy on treatment, after CAR-T, after HCT (1–2 years) |
| **G-CSF** | Lowers febrile neutropenia incidence and duration when risk >20%, but **not mortality** {JCO 2015;33:3199}. Consider if critically ill, prolonged neutropenia expected, PTLD or HIV. Caution in leukemia (discuss with attending) |
