# ACLS: Bradycardia
> Bradycardia with a pulse: HR <60 and symptomatic. If unstable, give atropine and have a low threshold to pace. If stable, find and reverse the cause. Always think of hyperkalemia. {Circ 2020 Evidence Review; ACC/AHA Bradycardia 2018}

## Assess first
- **Focused exam:** vitals, mental status, pupils, pulmonary edema, murmurs, warm vs cold
- **Review:** recent ECG, telemetry, labs, meds and recent dose changes
- **Obtain:** 12-lead ECG; pacing pads available; call for atropine to the bedside early (it can take time)
- **IV access:** BMP, Mg, lactate ± troponin
- **Monitor BP** frequently; keep SpO2 >92%; maintain the airway

## Unstable: treat now
:::warn Unstable if any of these are present
- Hypotension / shock
- Altered mental status
- Ischemic chest discomfort
- Acute heart failure / pulmonary edema
:::
### 1. Medications (low threshold to move on to pacing)
- **Atropine 1 mg IV bolus** q3–5 min, max 3 mg (half-life 2 h)
  - Do not give after heart transplant
  - Will not help Mobitz II or complete heart block (block is below the AV node) and may worsen Mobitz II by raising the atrial rate
- **Dopamine** infusion 5–20 mcg/kg/min **or** **epinephrine** infusion 2–10 mcg/min
- Give a **specific antidote** if a drug is the cause (below)

### 2. Pacing
- **Transcutaneous:** lorazepam 2 mg + hydromorphone 2 mg → turn defibrillator to PACER → set rate 100 bpm and output 100 mA → lower output to the minimum that still captures → adjust rate to hemodynamics
- **Transvenous wire** (placed by cardiology) if transcutaneous pacing is needed despite medications, or the rhythm is unstable with no quickly reversible cause

## Stable: monitor and find the cause
- Monitor and look for reversible causes
- **Hold** AV nodal blockers and antihypertensives
- **Mobitz II or complete heart block with a wide QRS** often gets a pacing wire even when stable: call cardiology and be ready to pace

| Mostly sinus bradycardia | Mostly complete heart block |
|---|---|
| Sick sinus syndrome | After TAVR |
| Elevated ICP | After septal ablation |
| Hypothermia, hypothyroidism | Endocarditis |
| Hypoxia, sleep apnea | Degenerative/calcific conduction disease |
| Carotid disease, recent carotid stent | Infiltrative disease; Lyme, autoimmune myocarditis; pericarditis |

**Either:** ischemia/ACS (often inferior MI), high vagal tone, meds/toxins (especially with liver or kidney disease), hyperkalemia.

## Name the rhythm
Rhythms in **bold** → call cardiology to consider pacing.
::: P waves present, regular P–P, regular P–R | Same number of P waves and QRS complexes, or a fixed pattern
- Sinus bradycardia (one P per QRS)
- Mobitz I with 2:1 block
- **Mobitz II** (more P waves than QRS)
:::
::: P waves present, regular P–P, irregular P–R | P–R changes from beat to beat
- Mobitz I (more P waves than QRS)
- **Complete heart block** with junctional escape, or ventricular escape if the QRS is wide (P waves unrelated to QRS)
:::
::: P waves present, irregular P–P | Atrial rate itself is irregular
- Sinus pause / sinus arrhythmia
- Wandering atrial pacemaker
- Non-conducted PACs
:::
::: No P waves | Junctional or ventricular escape, or AF
- **Sinus arrest** with junctional escape, or ventricular (idioventricular) escape
- AF/flutter with slow or variable conduction
:::
:::tip Pearls
- Grouped beats → look for Mobitz I or II
- With 2:1 AV block you cannot tell Mobitz I from Mobitz II
- Always consider hyperkalemia
:::

## Antidotes by cause
| Cause | Antidote |
|---|---|
| Beta-blocker | Glucagon 3–10 mg IV; repeat bolus if no response; if it works, infuse 3–5 mg/h |
| Calcium channel blocker | Glucagon as above; calcium gluconate 3–6 g q10–20 min or drip; insulin 1 U/kg bolus then 0.5 U/kg/h |
| Digoxin | Digoxin immune Fab: 1 vial binds ~0.5 mg digoxin; give over 30 min |
| Opioids | Naloxone 0.4–0.8 mg IV; consider a drip |
| Organophosphate | Atropine 2–5 mg IV, doubling the dose q5 min until effect; pralidoxime 1–2 g IV over 15–30 min |

{Resuscitation 1999;41:47; Am J Cardiol 1974;33:333}
