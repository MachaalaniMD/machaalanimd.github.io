# Magnesium & Phosphorus Disorders
> Low Mg makes low K and low Ca impossible to fix; replete it first. Treat phosphate <1 (or falling fast, as in refeeding) with IV; otherwise oral. High Mg and acute high phosphate are almost always kidney problems.

## Low magnesium {Med Sci (Basel) 2019;7:56; AJKD 2024;803:815}
- **Effects:** low K, low Ca, alkalosis, hypoparathyroidism, lethargy, weakness. Severe: delirium, coma, hyperreflexia, tetany, seizures; ECG widening QRS and peaked T → long PR and QTc, U waves, VT/torsades; worse digoxin toxicity.
- **GI causes:** poor intake (alcohol use disorder), diarrhea, pancreatitis, malabsorption, small bowel resection, **PPIs**, refeeding
- **Kidney wasting:** thiazides, loops, amphotericin B, aminoglycosides, foscarnet, tacrolimus/cyclosporine, cisplatin, digoxin, pentamidine; Gitelman; type 2 diabetes; alcohol
- **FEMg >3%** means kidney wasting.

| Situation | Replete |
|---|---|
| **Unstable** (arrhythmia, seizure) | MgSO4 1–2 g IV over 2–15 min, ×2 |
| **Symptomatic or inpatient** | MgSO4 1–2 g IV over 30–60 min; reduce if CrCl <30 |
| **Stable outpatient** | MgCl2 4–8 tabs/day (less diarrhea than Mg oxide 800–1600 mg/day), divided doses |

Slow repletion lasts longer (fast IV is partly excreted). **Kidney wasting:** add a K-sparing diuretic or SGLT2 inhibitor.

## High magnesium {AJKD 2024;803:815}
- **Usually only symptomatic if Mg >4.** Mild: nausea, flushing, headache, drowsiness. Severe: **loss of reflexes (first sign)**, weakness, respiratory failure; hypotension, bradycardia, long PR/QRS/QTc, heart block, arrest; low Ca (PTH suppression).
- **Causes:** intake beyond kidney clearance; rare without kidney disease. Mg-containing laxatives, antacids, Epsom salts, enemas (avoid in ESRD), pre-eclampsia treatment; gastritis, ulcers or colitis (more absorption); DKA, tumor lysis, lithium, adrenal insufficiency, milk-alkali syndrome, over-repletion.
- **Treat if symptomatic:** calcium gluconate 1 g IV over 10 min (or drip) for respiratory or cardiac toxicity; IV fluids + loop diuretic; **HD in ESRD.**

## Low phosphate {AJKD 2024;250:253; JCEM 2012;696:706}
- **Effects** (usually <1 mg/dL, especially acute): ATP depletion → weakness, encephalopathy, paresthesias, seizures, heart failure, respiratory failure, proximal myopathy, rhabdo, dysphagia/ileus, hemolysis, bone pain and demineralization. {JASN 2007;18:1999}
- **Shift into cells:** insulin (treating DKA/HHS, refeeding), acute respiratory alkalosis, hungry bone after parathyroidectomy
- **Low absorption:** poor intake, chronic diarrhea, low vitamin D, antacids and binders (aluminum, Mg, sevelamer)
- **Kidney losses:** high PTH, Fanconi (myeloma, tenofovir), high FGF-23 (genetic, tumor-induced), acetazolamide, metolazone, **IV iron** {QJM 2010;103:449}, glycosuria, CVVH (especially high bicarbonate), after kidney transplant
- **FEPO4 >5%** means kidney wasting.

| Situation | Replete |
|---|---|
| **Severe** (<1, or 1–2 with high risk such as refeeding) or symptomatic | IV Na or K phosphate 30 mmol q6h with frequent levels; switch to oral once >1.5. Half dose in CKD/ESRD. Fast IV can precipitate Ca (low Ca, tetany, seizures, arrhythmia, hypotension) and cause AKI |
| **Asymptomatic** (<2, not high risk) | Oral 1 mmol/kg/day in 3–4 doses (max 80 mmol/day) |

## Acute high phosphate
For chronic hyperphosphatemia, see *Chronic Kidney Disease*.
- **Effects:** Ca precipitation → low Ca: cramps, tetany, tingling, perioral numbness
- **Causes:** acute load (tumor lysis, rhabdo, phosphate laxatives, fosphenytoin); shift out of cells (DKA, lactic acidosis, severe hyperglycemia); AKI/CKD (including acute phosphate nephropathy); vitamin D toxicity; **pseudohyperphosphatemia** (paraproteins, lipemia, hyperbilirubinemia, hemolysis)
- **Treat:** NS (can worsen low Ca), HD; low-phosphate diet, binders (sevelamer); renal consult if refractory or severe
