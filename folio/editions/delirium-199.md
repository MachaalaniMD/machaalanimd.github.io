# Delirium
> An acute, fluctuating disturbance of attention: a vulnerable brain plus an acute insult. Find and treat the cause, prevent with sleep, mobility and reorientation, and reserve antipsychotics for dangerous behavior.

## Recognize it
:::key DSM-5-TR: all four
1. **Disturbed attention** (can't focus, sustain or shift it) and awareness
2. **Another cognitive change:** memory, orientation, language, visuospatial, perception
3. **Acute** (hours to days), a change from baseline, and **fluctuating**
4. Not better explained by another neurocognitive disorder, and not in the setting of coma or severely reduced arousal
:::
- **4AT** (on MDCalc) screens alertness, attention, orientation and acute change; sensitivity and specificity 80–89%. {Age Ageing 2021;50:733}
- **Why it matters:** higher mortality {JAMA 2010;304:433}, more institutionalization {Lancet 2014;383:911}, lasting cognitive decline. {NEJM 2012;367:30}

## Find the cause
**Vulnerable brain:** older age, stroke, dementia, TBI, vascular disease/HTN, substance use disorder, impaired vision or hearing, psychiatric illness.

:::warn Rule out the dangerous causes first (WWHHHHHIIMMPS)
- **W**ithdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines) · **W**ernicke
- **H**ypoxia (MI, heart failure, anemia) · **H**ypoglycemia · **H**ypoperfusion of the brain (vascular disease, after surgery) · **H**yper/hypothermia · **H**ypertensive encephalopathy
- **I**ntracerebral hemorrhage · **I**nfection
- **M**eningitis/encephalitis · **M**etabolic (kidney or liver failure, DKA, acid–base, adrenal)
- **P**oisoning (heavy metals, anticholinergics, drug interactions)
- **S**eizures / nonconvulsive status
:::
Also: systemic inflammation (leakier blood–brain barrier), big swings in HR or BP, intoxication (including from hospital meds), endocrine problems, vitamin deficiencies, toxins.

## Prevent it
- **Cut deliriogenic meds:** anticholinergics, antihistamines, benzodiazepines, opioids where possible
- **Basics:** no urinary retention, regular bowel movements, good pain control
- **Environment:** reorient often; mobilize with PT/OT and out of bed to chair; glasses and hearing aids on; fewer lines, telemetry leads and catheters; early volume repletion if dry; **avoid room changes and restraints** {JAMA 2017;318:1161}
- **Sleep:** standing melatonin 3 mg at 6 PM; lights on by day and off at night; give meds earlier in the evening; no late diuresis; less noise
- **Suvorexant** has better evidence than trazodone for prevention

## Manage it
1. **Treat the underlying cause.** Keep IV access if you can.
2. **Non-drug care first:** delirium precautions, fewer deliriogenic meds, fix sleep–wake cycles
3. **Prepare for medications:** daily QTc (goal <550); keep K >4 and Mg >2
4. **Medications only for danger to self or others.** They don't shorten delirium {NEJM 2018;379:2506} or reduce severity or length of stay {JAGS 2016;64:705}, and add QTc and interaction risks.

**Escalation:** behavioral/environmental care >> 1:1 sitter (reorients) >> medication >> restraints (which worsen delirium).

### Hyperactive delirium {NEJM 2017;377:1456}
Start low and PRN; schedule only if needed.
| Drug | PRN starting dose | If >2 PRNs needed (consider psychiatry) | Profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haloperidol** | 0.25–0.5 mg IV q30–60 min; or 0.5–1 mg PO q4h; or IM q1h | Double the PRN dose q20 min until effective (~5–20 mg IV) | Least sedating; IV causes less EPS than PO; most QTc prolongation |
| **Olanzapine** | 2.5–5 mg SL/PO/IM daily to q4h | Standing 2.5–10 mg BID + extra dose at bedtime | Most sedating; lowest EPS |
| **Quetiapine** | 12.5–25 mg PO q1h | Standing 25–50 mg TID + extra dose at bedtime | PO only |

- **QTc risk:** haloperidol > quetiapine > olanzapine. Change drugs if QTc rises 25–50%, exceeds 500, or U waves / T-wave flattening appear.
- Stop as soon as possible: prolonged antipsychotic use raises mortality in older adults.
- **Avoid benzodiazepines** (they worsen delirium), unless withdrawal risk outweighs that.
- Prolonged QTc or refractory symptoms → discuss with psychiatry.

## When to consult
- **Psychiatry:** escalating or persistent delirium; neurodegenerative disease (especially Parkinson), TBI, psychiatric illness or substance use disorder; significant cardiac disease or critical illness. Have the work-up so far and current/past meds ready.
- **Neurology:** know the last-known-well time, baseline deficits and anticoagulation status.
