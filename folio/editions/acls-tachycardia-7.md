# ACLS: Tachycardia
> Tachycardia with a pulse: HR >120 and symptomatic. Decide whether the patient is unstable; if so, shock. If not, sort the rhythm by QRS width and regularity, then treat. {ACLS Guidelines 2020}

## Assess first
- **Focused exam:** vitals, mental status, pulmonary edema, murmurs, warm vs cold, pupils, other symptoms
- **Review:** most recent ECG, telemetry, labs, meds, events
- **Obtain:** 12-lead ECG; defibrillator with pads on
- **IV access:** BMP, Mg, lactate ± troponin if concern for ischemia
  - Correct all electrolyte abnormalities
- **Monitor BP** frequently
- **O2:** supplement to SpO2 >92%; maintain the airway

## Is the patient unstable?
:::warn Unstable if any of these are present
- Hypotension / shock
- Altered mental status
- Ischemic chest discomfort / angina
- Acute heart failure / pulmonary edema
:::
**Yes → electrical therapy now.** Give analgesia (hydromorphone 1–2 mg) + sedation (lorazepam 2 mg) if time allows.

| Rhythm | Mode | Energy (J, biphasic) |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow & regular | Synchronized | 50–100 |
| Narrow & irregular | Synchronized | 120–200 |
| Wide & regular | Synchronized | 100 |
| Wide & irregular | **Defibrillate** | 120–200 |

**No → medications,** chosen by QRS width (≥ or <120 ms) and regularity.

## Stable: sort the rhythm
::: Narrow & regular | QRS <120 ms, regular
1. Vagal maneuvers (see below)
2. **Adenosine** (avoid if heart transplant)
3. IV beta-blocker or diltiazem (avoid diltiazem in HFrEF), or amiodarone / procainamide

**Differential:** sinus tachycardia, atrial tachycardia, orthodromic AVRT, AVNRT, atrial flutter.
If P waves are hard to see, adenosine can slow the rate enough to tell them apart.
:::
::: Narrow & irregular | QRS <120 ms, irregular
1. **Oral vs IV beta-blocker or diltiazem** (avoid diltiazem in HFrEF). Choose by rate, need for a quick effect, and concern for hypotension.
2. If it persists or BP is borderline: **amiodarone** (rhythm and rate; may cardiovert, so consider anticoagulation status) or **digoxin** (rate).
3. Consider empiric Mg 2 g.

**Differential:** AF with RVR, MAT, atrial flutter/atrial tachycardia with variable block, sinus tachycardia with frequent PACs.
Consider chronicity and anticoagulation status before giving rhythm control for AF/flutter.
:::
::: Wide & regular | QRS ≥120 ms, regular
1. **Amiodarone** (safe for VT or SVT)
2. Then by EF:
   - **EF >40%:** lidocaine, sotalol, procainamide, or metoprolol
   - **EF <40%:** lidocaine
- If you suspect SVT with aberrant conduction, you can trial vagal maneuvers + adenosine; it will not worsen VT.

**Differential:** monomorphic VT, SVT with aberrant conduction, antidromic AVRT, pre-excitation, meds and electrolyte abnormalities.
:::
::: Wide & irregular | QRS ≥120 ms, irregular
1. **Lidocaine + Mg 2 g** (over 15 min), and treat myocardial ischemia
2. Then by baseline QTc:
   - **Prolonged QTc → torsades (TdP):** Mg 2–4 g and raise the heart rate (overdrive pacing, isoproterenol)
   - **Normal QTc → non-TdP polymorphic VT:** second line amiodarone or procainamide
- **Pre-excited AF (SVT):** procainamide; AV nodal blockers are contraindicated
- Lidocaine is generally safe: effective for all polymorphic VT and will not worsen pre-excited rhythms

**Differential:** polymorphic VT (TdP and non-TdP), AF/flutter with pre-excitation vs aberrancy.
:::

## Drug doses
| Drug | Dose | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Adenosine** | 6 mg rapid IV push + NS flush, raise the arm; may repeat once at 12 mg | Central line: use half the dose. Relative contraindication after heart transplant |
| **Amiodarone** | 150 mg IV over 10 min (may repeat ×1), then 1 mg/min ×6 h, then 0.5 mg/min ×18 h | Max 2.2 g/24 h |
| **Diltiazem** | 0.25 mg/kg IV over 2 min, then drip | Avoid if LVEF unknown |
| **Lidocaine** | 1–1.5 mg/kg IV bolus (usually 100 mg); if refractory 0.5–0.75 mg/kg q5–10 min PRN, max 3 mg/kg; maintenance 1–4 mg/min | Preferred if QTc is prolonged |
| **Metoprolol** | 2.5–5 mg IV over 2 min; repeat q5 min, max 15 mg | Start low; follow with oral after IV rate control |
| **Procainamide** | 20–50 mg/min or 100 mg q5 min until the arrhythmia stops, BP falls, QRS widens 50%, or 17 mg/kg given; maintenance 1–4 mg/min (adjust for CrCl) | Avoid if QTc is prolonged |
| **Sotalol** | 1–1.5 mg/kg IV over 5 min, then 10 mg/min | Contraindicated if QTc prolonged; caution if low EF |

## Vagal maneuvers
- **Modified Valsalva (best):** semi-recumbent, blow forcefully into a 10 mL syringe for 10–15 s, then lie flat and passively raise the legs to 45° for 15 s. Breaks 43% of SVTs vs 17% with standard Valsalva. {Lancet 2015;386:1747}
- **Carotid sinus massage:** supine, neck extended; steady pressure below the angle of the jaw at the level of the thyroid cartilage. Avoid with TIA/stroke in the past 3 months or carotid bruits. 5–33% success.
- **Cold face immersion** or ice-water bag to the face (diving reflex; works better in children): 17% success.
