# Community-Acquired Pneumonia
> New infiltrate plus a compatible illness. Triage with CURB-65 or PSI, pick empiric antibiotics by setting and resistance risk, and treat for 5 days once stable (7 for MRSA or Pseudomonas). {ATS/IDSA, AJRCCM 2019;200:45}

## Diagnose
- **CAP** = pneumonia acquired in the community, including nursing home, dialysis and clinic exposure
- **Diagnosis:** new infiltrate on imaging **and** symptoms or signs (fever, cough, leukocytosis, changed sputum, hypoxemia)
- Older adults often have blunted symptoms, but also more atelectasis and aspiration.
- Imaging can't tell bacterial from viral. Neutropenic patients may have a normal CXR.

**Usual organisms:** S. pneumoniae and respiratory viruses > S. aureus, H. influenzae, gram-negative rods, Legionella. In severe CAP: S. pneumoniae, S. aureus, Pseudomonas, Legionella. {NEJM 2015;358:415; Crit Care Med 2022;50(7)}

## Where to treat
- **CURB-65** (Confusion, BUN >19, RR >29, SBP <90 or DBP <60, age >65): score 0–1 → outpatient
- **PSI** (more detailed): <70 outpatient; >90 inpatient

:::warn Severe CAP (ATS/IDSA)
**1 major:** needs vasopressors or mechanical ventilation
**or 3 minor:** RR >30, P/F <250, multilobar infiltrates, confusion, BUN >19, WBC <4 (not from chemo), platelets <100, T <36 °C, hypotension needing aggressive fluids
:::

## Empiric antibiotics {IDSA/ATS; immunocompromised hosts need more: Chest 2022;158:1896}
| Setting | Preferred | Alternatives / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Outpatient, healthy, no MRSA/Pseudomonas risk** | Amoxicillin 1 g TID **or** doxycycline 100 mg BID **or** a macrolide (only if local resistance <25%) | US rates of macrolide- and doxycycline-resistant pneumococcus are high |
| **Outpatient with comorbidities*** | Amoxicillin-clavulanate BID + azithromycin, **or** levofloxacin alone | Cefpodoxime or cefuroxime for amox-clav; doxycycline for azithromycin |
| **Inpatient, not severe** | Ceftriaxone + azithromycin, **or** levofloxacin alone | Ampicillin-sulbactam for ceftriaxone; doxycycline if macrolide and fluoroquinolone not tolerated |
| **Severe / ICU** | Ceftriaxone 1–2 g daily + azithromycin (preferred in the ICU for its anti-inflammatory effect) or levofloxacin | Add resistant coverage if risk factors (below) |
| **MRSA or Pseudomonas risk** | Vancomycin + cefepime | Get cultures and MRSA nasal swab to de-escalate. Never daptomycin (inactivated in the lung) |

*Chronic heart, lung, liver or kidney disease; diabetes; alcohol use disorder; malignancy; asplenia.

### Who needs resistant coverage?
- **Anyone:** hospitalized with IV antibiotics in the past 90 days, or prior respiratory MRSA, Pseudomonas or other resistant organism
- **Pseudomonas:** gram-negative rods on Gram stain, prior Pseudomonas, bronchiectasis, COPD with frequent antibiotic/steroid courses → cefepime, ceftazidime, pip-tazo or a carbapenem. Double coverage usually isn't needed.
- **MRSA:** gram-positive cocci in clusters, recent flu-like illness, necrotizing pneumonia, cavitation or empyema, positive nasal swab, colonization risk (ESRD, injection drug use, prior antibiotics, especially fluoroquinolones) → vancomycin or linezolid
- **Anaerobes:** only for suspected empyema or lung abscess (see *HAP/VAP & Aspiration Pneumonia*)

## Tests (inpatients)
| Test | When / how to read it |
|---|---|
| **Sputum culture + Gram stain** (ET aspirate if intubated) | Severe CAP or resistance risk. Adequate if >25 PMNs and <10 squamous cells; "abundant squamous cells" means saliva |
| **Blood cultures** | Positive in only <10–20% of inpatients. Send both sputum and blood if severe, treating empirically for MRSA/Pseudomonas, prior MRSA/Pseudomonas, or hospitalized with IV antibiotics ≤90 days |
| **MRSA nasal swab** | NPV ~98%: if negative, consider stopping MRSA coverage {CID 2018;67:1} |
| **Legionella urine antigen** | Severe CAP, recent travel or exposure. Sn 70%, Sp 99%; detects serogroup 1 only (80–90% of US cases). Clues: hyponatremia, fever, diarrhea, travel {CID 2019;68:2026} |
| **Influenza** | Test in season; treat with oseltamivir (see *Viral Respiratory Infections*) |
| **Procalcitonin** | Higher in bacterial infection, but no validated cutoff and no clear role in deciding whether to start antibiotics {CID 2020;70:538} |

## Duration and response
- **5 days** usually; **7 days** for suspected or proven MRSA or Pseudomonas, once clinically stable
- **Stable** = afebrile 48 h and ≤1 of: HR >100, RR >24, SpO2 <90%, AMS, not eating
- **MRSA not improving by 72 h:** treat longer (10–14 days if critically ill; 2–6 weeks with ID for bacteremia or necrotizing pneumonia)
- **Not stable?** Extend and look for a resistant organism, a complication (empyema, abscess) or another source
- **Switch IV → oral** once improving; no need to watch 24 h on oral

**Expected course:** tachycardia resolves by days 2–3, fever by 2–4, hypoxemia by 3–6. CXR clears in 50% by 1 month (up to 12 weeks in older adults or with lung disease). No repeat CXR needed if improving. **No response by 72 h:** chest CT ± BAL for empyema, abscess or fungal infection.

## Steroids
- Lowered mortality in **severe** bacterial CAP. {NEJM 2023;388:1931}
- Meta-analysis: may reduce mechanical ventilation, no clear mortality effect. {Chest 2023;163:484} IDSA/ATS make no recommendation.
- **Avoid in influenza:** may raise mortality. {Cochrane Rev 2016}

## Parapneumonic effusion
Occurs in 20–57% of pneumonia admissions. {CID 2007;45:1480} Tap any sizable effusion, especially if infection is possible or it would change antibiotic duration.
| Type | Fluid | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncomplicated** | pH >7.2, glucose >60, Gram stain/culture negative | Antibiotics alone if < ½ hemithorax and free-flowing; therapeutic tap if symptomatic |
| **Complicated** | pH <7.2, low glucose, Gram stain/culture negative | Drain |
| **Empyema** | Frank pus or positive Gram stain/culture | Drain |

**Chest tube** after the diagnostic tap for: empyema, loculations, > ½ hemithorax, thickened parietal pleura, or sepsis with a suspected pleural source. {Chest 2000;118:1158; Ann Thorac Surg 2018;105:1589} Duration, surgery and repeat imaging depend on severity and progress.
