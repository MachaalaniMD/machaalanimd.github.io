# Sepsis
> A medical emergency: antibiotics within 1 hour, ~30 mL/kg crystalloid within 3 hours, norepinephrine to MAP >65, and find the source. {Surviving Sepsis 2021, Crit Care Med 2021;49:e1063}

## First hour
:::key The four things to do now
1. **Cultures, then antibiotics:** at least 2 sets of blood cultures, then broad-spectrum IV antibiotics ordered STAT, ideally within 1 hour
2. **Fluids:** crystalloid ~30 mL/kg within 3 hours (more caution with heart or kidney failure); prefer balanced fluid (LR)
3. **Vasopressors** if still hypotensive: norepinephrine first, target MAP >65
4. **Source control:** look for anything that needs draining, removing or operating on
:::
- Each hour of antibiotic delay raises mortality ~7.6%. {CCM 2006;34:1589; CCM 2010;38:1045; NEJM 2017;376:2235}
- Don't delay antibiotics to get cultures if drawing them would cause a significant delay.

## Definitions
- **Sepsis:** life-threatening organ dysfunction (SOFA rise ≥2) from a dysregulated response to infection {Sepsis-3, JAMA 2016;315:801}
- **Septic shock:** sepsis needing vasopressors for MAP >65 **and** lactate >2 despite adequate volume
- **SOFA** includes PaO2/FiO2, platelets, bilirubin, BP, GCS, creatinine/urine output
- **qSOFA** (RR >22, altered mentation, SBP ≤100): a score ≥2 outside the ICU predicts longer ICU stay and higher mortality, but guidelines advise against using it alone as a screening tool. {Crit Care Med 2021;49:e1063}
- SIRS can be an appropriate host response; SIRS + infection is not sensitive for sepsis. {NEJM 2015;372:1629}

## Antibiotics
- Choose by suspected site, prior cultures, local resistance, exposures (SNF, lines, recent antibiotics) and immune status. See *Empiric Antimicrobials*.
- **Double gram-negative coverage** (β-lactam + fluoroquinolone or aminoglycoside) if septic shock with high risk of resistant organisms, e.g. neutropenia or burns. {J Infect Dis 2020;222:S119}
- **ESBL** outside the urinary tract: carbapenem over pip-tazo, even if susceptible. {JAMA 2018;320:984}
- **Toxic shock** suspected: add clindamycin or linezolid (anti-toxin effect, extra staph/strep coverage).
- **Invasive Candida risk** (neutropenia, chemotherapy, transplant, lines, TPN, recent major abdominal surgery, long stay or antibiotic course): consider empiric micafungin.
- **β-lactams:** after the loading dose, use extended or continuous infusion. {Lancet Infect Dis 2018;18:108}
- **De-escalate** to the narrowest effective agent once the organism is known; procalcitonin can help decide when to stop. {Lancet 2010;375:463; Lancet ID 2016;16:819; CCM 2018;46:684}

## Fluids
- **Balanced crystalloid** (LR) lowers mortality and kidney injury vs NS in critically ill adults. {NEJM 2018;378:829} Colloids add no mortality benefit. {NEJM 2004;350:2247; JAMA 2013;310:1809; NEJM 2014;370:1412}
- **Targets:** lactate clearance (normal, or falling >20% per 2 h) {JAMA 2010;303:739}; capillary refill <3 s {JAMA 2019;321:654}
- **How much:** restrictive and liberal strategies give similar mortality {NEJM 2023;388:499}; use judgment to avoid volume overload.
- Isotonic bicarbonate may lower ARDS and multiorgan dysfunction in hemorrhagic shock, without clear mortality benefit. {AmJEM 2024;76:41; Medicine 2022;101(46):e31671}

### Is the patient fluid responsive? {JAMA 2016;316:1298}
| Test | How | Responsive if |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse pressure variation | Only if ventilated with VT ≥8 mL/kg, not triggering, in sinus rhythm. PPV = (PPmax − PPmin) / PPmean {Crit Care 2014;18:650} | PPV ≥13% |
| Passive leg raise | Legs to 45°, torso flat, for 1 min (autotransfuses ~250–350 mL) | Pulse pressure (or SV) rises ≥10% |
| Fluid challenge | Bolus 250–500 mL; CVP before and right after (a rise ≥2 = adequate challenge) | Better BP, urine output, lactate; pulse pressure up ≥10% |

## Vasopressors
Target **MAP >65** {NEJM 2014;370:1583}. Permissive hypotension (MAP >60) uses less vasopressor and may suit select patients. {JAMA 2020;323:938} See *Vasopressors*.
| Agent | When |
|---|---|
| **Norepinephrine** | First choice |
| **Vasopressin** | Add when norepinephrine reaches 5–15 mcg/min (helps kidney function). Fixed at 0.04 U/min, not titrated {NEJM 2008;358:877} |
| **Epinephrine** | Second or third agent; consider when norepinephrine is climbing past 25 mcg/min |
| **Phenylephrine** | Norepinephrine causing serious arrhythmias; high cardiac output with low BP; norepinephrine + vasopressin failing at >25 mcg/min with marked tachycardia; hypotension with AF with RVR |
| **Dopamine** | Only for select patients with bradycardia and low arrhythmia risk; more arrhythmias and deaths in all-comers {CCM 2012;40:725; NEJM 2010;362:779} |
| **Angiotensin II, methylene blue, hydroxocobalamin** | Refractory distributive shock {ATHOS-3, NEJM 2017;377:419}. Methylene blue: hemolysis with G6PD deficiency; serotonin syndrome with serotonergic drugs |

## Find the source
- **Blood cultures:** at least 2 sets before antibiotics (higher yield {Annals 2019;171:547}), at least one drawn peripherally. Cultures drawn only from lines give many false positives; draw from a line only if you suspect a line infection (rigors with infusions, redness or induration at the site).
- Consider **β-D-glucan, galactomannan, cryptococcal antigen** if fungal infection is possible.
- **Needs source control:** necrotizing soft tissue infection, abscess, cholangitis, cholecystitis, GI perforation or ischemia, pyelonephritis with an obstructing stone or abscess, empyema, septic arthritis, infected devices.
- Once stable, low threshold for cross-sectional imaging.

## Steroids, kidneys, acid–base, blood
### Corticosteroids
- **Give** hydrocortisone 50 mg q6h or 100 mg q8h (≤400 mg/day) ± fludrocortisone 0.1 mg if shock persists on vasopressors (e.g. norepinephrine >15–25 mcg/min). {Crit Care Med 2021;49:e1063; Crit Care Med 2024}
- **Evidence is mixed:** mortality benefit with hydrocortisone 50 mg q6h + fludrocortisone 50 mcg/day ×7 days {JAMA 2002;288:862; NEJM 2018;378:809}; no difference in other trials {NEJM 2008;358:111; JAMA 2016;316:1775; NEJM 2018;378:797; ICM 2024;50:2050}. Adding fludrocortisone may help some outcomes. {JAMA Intern Med 2023;183:451}
- **Severe bacterial CAP:** steroids lowered mortality (CAPE COD). {NEJM 2023;388:1931}
- Rationale: critical illness can blunt the HPA axis (critical illness-related corticosteroid insufficiency).

### AKI
- Starting dialysis early (<12 h) vs delayed (>48 h) without an urgent indication does not change mortality. {NEJM 2018;379:1431; NEJM 2016;375:122; NEJM 2020;383:240}
- CVVH and HD are similar; CVVH causes fewer fluid shifts in unstable patients.

### Lactic / metabolic acidosis
- Fix the cause. Bicarbonate can bridge if **pH <7.1**, or **pH <7.2 with AKI** (bicarbonate drip to pH >7.3, ≤1 L/24 h, lowered mortality in BICAR-ICU). {Lancet 2018;392:31}
- 1 amp = 50 mEq in 50 mL (8.4%); or 150 mEq (3 amps) in 1 L D5W.
- Bicarbonate makes CO2 that must be breathed off (needs adequate ventilation or intubation) and lowers ionized calcium.

### Transfusion
- Hgb goal 7 g/dL unless cardiac ischemia or active bleeding. {NEJM 2014;371:1381}

## What doesn't help
- **Vitamin C:** higher mortality and organ dysfunction vs placebo. {NEJM 2022;386:2387}
- **Thiamine:** more ICU-free days, no mortality benefit. {AJRCCM 2023;208:570}
- **Short-acting β-blockers:** one RCT of esmolol (HR 80–94) lowered mortality, but the control group's mortality was 80.5% {JAMA 2013;310:1683}; landiolol showed no benefit (37.1% vs 28.6%, favoring standard care). {JAMA 2023;330:1641} Not routine.

## How sepsis injures organs
Microbial products activate immune cells and inflammatory mediators. When this spreads beyond the local site, microvascular thrombosis, stiff red cells, mitochondrial injury and leaky endothelium cause organ dysfunction. {NEJM 2013;369:840; BMJ 2016;353:i1585}
| Organ | Effect |
|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | Vasodilation and hypotension; ventricles hyperdynamic or depressed |
| Lungs | Pulmonary edema, ARDS |
| Gut | Leakier gut, bacterial translocation, more inflammation |
| Liver | Sepsis-induced cholestasis; impaired reticuloendothelial function |
| Kidneys | AKI from microvascular dysfunction, oxidative stress, hypoperfusion |
| Blood | Early inflammation, later immunosuppression; DIC, low platelets |
| Endocrine | Dysglycemia, adrenal dysfunction, sick euthyroid |
| Brain | Encephalopathy |
