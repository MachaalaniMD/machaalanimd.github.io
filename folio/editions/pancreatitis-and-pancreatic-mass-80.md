# Pancreatitis & Pancreatic Mass
> Diagnose with 2 of 3 (typical pain, lipase >3× normal, imaging). Give moderate fluids (LR), feed early, get a RUQ ultrasound in everyone, and remove the gallbladder before discharge in gallstone pancreatitis. {ACG: AJG 2024;119(3):e419; Revised Atlanta: Gut 2013;62:102}

## Diagnose
:::key 2 of 3
1. Typical pain: epigastric/LUQ (90%), band-like to the back (50%)
2. Lipase >3× ULN (no need to trend; false positives in CKD, DKA)
3. Typical imaging (ultrasound, contrast CT, MRI)
:::
- **Also:** nausea/vomiting (90%), ileus, jaundice, flank or periumbilical bruising
- **History:** prior episodes, alcohol, smoking, recent GI procedures (ERCP, cholecystectomy), new drugs, infection, autoimmune disease, family history
- **Labs:** CBC (Hct often high), BMP with Ca, LFTs (**ALT >3.5× ULN → 95% PPV for gallstones** {AJG 1994;89:1863}), triglycerides. Recheck Hct and BUN within 6–8 h.
- **Imaging:** RUQ ultrasound for everyone (gallstones). CT (90% sensitive) only if the diagnosis is unclear, or at ~48–72 h for complications. MRI/MRCP for necrosis, strictures, or stones with a negative ultrasound.

## Severity
| | Mild | Moderately severe | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | No organ failure, no complications | Organ failure resolving within 48 h, or local/systemic complications | Organ failure >48 h (22% mortality) |

- Organ failure: modified Marshall score. Prognosis: **BISAP** is quickest; Ranson and APACHE II are less practical.
- **Risk factors for severe disease:** abnormal vitals, age >55, BMI >30, BUN >20, Hct >44, pleural effusion or infiltrate, extra-pancreatic collections.

## Treat {WATERFALL, NEJM 2022;389:989}
- **Fluids: moderate, not aggressive.** LR 1.5 mL/kg/h for the first 6–12 h, plus 10 mL/kg boluses over 2 h if hypovolemic (urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h or SBP <90). Aim to keep Hct and BUN from rising; ~3 L in the first 24 h is typical. LR > NS (avoid LR with hypercalcemia). **Avoid aggressive fluids after 24–48 h** (abdominal compartment syndrome, intubation). Can stop at ~20 h if eating for >8 h.
- **Pain:** IV opioids.
- **No prophylactic antibiotics.**
- **Nutrition:** low-fat oral diet within 24–48 h as tolerated. Not eating by days 5–7 → continuous tube feeds (NG ≥ NJ). **Enteral > TPN** (gut barrier; TPN brings infections, organ failure, death).
- **Remove the cause:**
  - **Gallstones:** ERCP within 24 h for cholangitis or CBD obstruction. **Cholecystectomy before discharge** (delay means more biliary complications {Lancet 2015;386:1261}).
  - **Hypertriglyceridemia** (TG >500 with pancreatitis, or >1000 to prevent it): insulin drip 0.1–0.3 U/kg/h + D5, glucose q1h initially, TG q12h, goal <500 (may take days). No good evidence for apheresis. Once eating, fibrates first; lifestyle and lipid clinic at discharge.
  - **Hypercalcemia:** treat; **stop culprit drugs.**
- **Idiopathic:** repeat ultrasound and TG as an outpatient; after a second episode, cholecystectomy ± genetic testing.

## Causes {NEJM 2016;375:1972; AJG 2024;119(3):419}
| Cause | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Gallstones / sludge** (40–75%) | Most common in women |
| **Alcohol** (25–35%) | Most common in men |
| **Hypertriglyceridemia** (10%) | Suspect if TG >1000 |
| **Post-ERCP** (1–30% of ERCPs) | Rectal NSAIDs lower risk |
| **Drugs** (<5%) | Class Ia: ACEi, dapsone, furosemide, metronidazole, pentamidine, statins, sulfa, tetracycline, valproate, mesalamine. Ib: amiodarone, azathioprine/6-MP, dexamethasone. II: didanosine, estrogen, propofol, tamoxifen, HCTZ {CGH 2007;5:648} |
| **Anatomic** | Ampullary diverticulum/stenosis, duodenal stricture, tumor, pancreas divisum, parasites, foreign body |
| **Autoimmune** | Type 1 (high IgG4), type 2 (normal IgG4) {Am J Gastro 2018;113:1301} |
| **Other** | Hypercalcemia; genetic (CFTR, CTRC, PRSS1, SPINK1, A1AT); toxins (smoking, organophosphates, methanol, scorpion, brown recluse); infections (viral, Mycoplasma, Legionella, Leptospira, Salmonella, Aspergillus, Toxoplasma, Cryptosporidium, Ascaris); ischemia (vasculitis, shock, cholesterol emboli); trauma |
| **Idiopathic** | 10–25% |

## Complications {NEJM 2016;375:1972}
| | <4 weeks | >4 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| **Interstitial pancreatitis** | Acute peripancreatic fluid collection: resolves on its own | **Pseudocyst** (defined wall). EUS/FNA if unclear (high amylase). Drain if symptomatic, growing fast or infected |
| **Necrotizing pancreatitis** | Acute necrotic collection | **Walled-off necrosis.** Drain after 2–4 weeks if symptomatic or infected |

- **Infected necrosis:** ⅓ of necrosis; suspect at ~7–10 days (early enteral feeding lowers risk). Discuss CT-guided FNA; assume infection if blood cultures are positive or there's gas. Antibiotics: cefepime or ciprofloxacin + metronidazole, or pip-tazo. Drainage/necrosectomy urgently if unstable; otherwise delay >4 weeks on antibiotics.
- **Vascular:** splenic, portal or SMV thrombosis (anticoagulate if portal vein or bowel ischemia). **Pseudoaneurysm** (GDA or splenic artery eroding into a collection): suspect with falling Hgb, a growing collection or unexplained GI bleeding → arterial-phase CT → IR embolization before any drainage.
- **Systemic:** abdominal compartment syndrome (bladder pressure >20 with new organ failure), ARDS, low Ca, high glucose and TG, GI bleeding, AKI, DIC.

## Chronic pancreatitis {ACG: AJG 2020;115:322}
- Repeated attacks (especially alcohol and smoking) → fibrosis → chronic pain, exocrine failure (steatorrhea, weight loss, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency) and brittle diabetes. Lipase may be normal or low late.
- **Tests:** fecal fat, low stool elastase; CT/MRI with calcifications, duct dilation, atrophy. Higher pancreatic cancer risk; consider genetic testing.
- **Treat:** pancreatic enzymes, replete vitamins A, D, E, K, pain control.

## Pancreatic masses {AJG 2018;113:464; Gastro 2015;148:819}
- **Solid:** adenocarcinoma (85–90%), autoimmune pancreatitis, neuroendocrine (1–5%), primary lymphoma (<1%), metastases (melanoma, RCC)
- **Cystic:** inflammatory (pseudocyst, paraduodenal wall cyst) or neoplastic. Malignant potential: main-duct IPMN >> branch-duct IPMN > mucinous cystic = solid pseudopapillary > serous cystadenoma.
- **Imaging:** pancreas-protocol CT; EUS-FNA for lesions >2–3 cm (Sn 87%, Sp 96%); MRI for <2 cm or vascular involvement; PET-CT and MRCP for malignancy in IPMN (Sn 70%, Sp 92%)
- **Labs:** CA 19-9 (positive in 80% of pancreatic cancers; Sn 86%, Sp 87%), CEA (mucinous cysts), ANA and IgG4 (autoimmune)
