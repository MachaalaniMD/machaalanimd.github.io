# Seizures
> Status epilepticus is ≥5 minutes of seizure: lorazepam 4 mg IV, then a second-line load (levetiracetam, valproate or fosphenytoin) by 5–10 minutes, then intubation and anesthetics by 20. For a first seizure: look for a provoking cause, get MRI and EEG, and usually hold off on long-term AEDs.

## Status epilepticus
:::warn Act on the clock
**Status** = ≥5 min of continuous seizure, or ≥2 seizures without full recovery between. {Epilepsia 2015;56:1515}
1. **0–5 min:** lorazepam 4 mg IV, repeat once at 5 min. Order the second-line drug now.
2. **>5 min:** call neurology; **load** one of: levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (max 4500 mg), valproate 40 mg/kg (max 3000 mg), or fosphenytoin 20 PE/kg over 10 min. These are equally effective. {ESETT, NEJM 2019;381:2103}
3. **>20 min:** intubate; propofol or midazolam infusion (ketamine third line). **Avoid etomidate.**
- **No IV:** midazolam 10 mg IM, intranasal or buccal, or diazepam 20 mg PR. Second-line drugs need an IV.
- **Possible eclampsia:** magnesium 4 g IV, consider 2 g/h; page OB.
:::
- **Nonconvulsive status:** electrographic seizures, or EEG and clinical improvement with treatment. {Epilepsy Behav 2015;49:158}

### Seizures that aren't status
Treat a generalized tonic-clonic seizure lasting >2–3 min, or clusters of focal seizures within 24 h: **lorazepam 1–2 mg IV** (1 mg if older or small), repeat once if needed. Discuss an AED load with neurology.

## Work-up
- **History:** prior seizures, prodrome, triggers (exertion, pain, sleep loss, stress, cough, urination/defecation, infection), lateral tongue bite, incontinence, lateralizing signs, alcohol/benzodiazepines, meds that lower the threshold (penicillins, opioids, antipsychotics, bupropion IR >300 mg {FDA 2023}). **Get collateral; if you witness it, video it** for neurology.
- **Labs:** fingerstick glucose, urine/serum tox, UA, CXR, AED levels, BMP/Na, Mg/phosphate, CBC, LFTs, VBG, CK, INR, lactate, β-hCG. **Don't send prolactin** (only rises 10–20 min after, and a normal level rules out nothing).
- **Monitor:** telemetry (ictal bradycardia/asystole) and pulse oximetry. {NEJM 2013;368:2304}
- **MRI** (epilepsy protocol, with contrast) for a first unprovoked seizure, focal seizures or exam, trauma, cancer or HIV; ~10% show a relevant lesion. {Continuum 2022;28:230; Neurology 2007;69:1996}
- **LP and blood cultures** if febrile, HIV/immunocompromised, or possible autoimmune encephalitis (anti-NMDA). {Epilepsia 2020;61:1341}
- **EEG** within 24–48 h (emergent if still seizing). Don't wait for it to treat. Routine EEG is ~50% sensitive in the first 24 h; a normal EEG doesn't rule out a convincing seizure.

**Provoked causes:** vascular (ischemia, hemorrhage), withdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines), masses (tumor, abscess), trauma, metabolic (glucose, high CO2, low O2, low Ca, low/high Na), meds, infection (systemic, CNS), hyper/hypotension, high fever, eclampsia, PRES.

**Differential:** syncope, TIA, migraine, PNES (~30% also have epilepsy {Brain Behav 2022;12}), myoclonus, dystonia, cataplexy, tremor, catatonia.

## Definitions {Epilepsia 2017;58:522; Continuum 2019;25:306; Continuum 2022;28:230; Lancet Neurol 2023;23:19}
- **Focal onset:** one hemisphere, ± impaired awareness, may become bilateral tonic-clonic
- **Generalized onset:** both hemispheres quickly; impaired awareness (frontal seizures can be bilateral with preserved awareness)
- **Motor:** tonic, clonic, myoclonic, atonic, automatisms. **Non-motor:** autonomic, staring/behavior change, cognitive, sensory.
- **Epilepsy:** ≥2 unprovoked seizures >24 h apart; or 1 unprovoked seizure with ≥60% 10-year recurrence risk; or an epilepsy syndrome
- **PNES** (functional seizures): long, fluctuating, side-to-side or asynchronous movements, eyes closed, preserved awareness and memory

## Prophylaxis and follow-up {Neurology 2015;84:1705; Neurology 2006;67:s45; Cochrane 2008;CD004424; Neurosurg Focus 2008;25:E3; Stroke 2016;47:2666}
- **First seizure:** no AED unless prior TBI, abnormal EEG or abnormal imaging. Early AEDs reduce recurrence for ~2 years but don't change long-term remission.
- **Alcohol:** no prophylaxis for intoxication or withdrawal seizures.
- **ICH:** only for a clinical seizure or traumatic cause; levetiracetam 500 mg BID × 7 days.
- **Severe TBI:** levetiracetam 500–750 mg BID × 7 days.
- **Brain tumor:** no prophylaxis; if seizures, levetiracetam > lacosamide (fewer chemo interactions).
- **PNES:** explain functional neurological disorder; social work; outpatient CBT and psychiatry.
- **Driving:** in Massachusetts, no driving until 6 months event-free (rules vary by state). Counsel the patient and document it.
