# Anticoagulation Management
> Which anticoagulant for which patient, and what to do around procedures. Most patients on a DOAC need no bridge; bridging warfarin is for high thrombotic risk only, because it adds bleeding without preventing strokes in typical AF.

## Choosing an agent
{CHEST VTE: Chest 2021;160:2247 · ASH VTE: Blood Adv 2021;5:927 · ASCO VTE in cancer: JCO 2020;38:496 · AF: JACC 2019;74:104; Chest 2018;154:1121 · Valves: JACC 2021;77:e25}
| Indication | Situation | Choice |
|---|---|---|
| **VTE** | Most patients | DOAC > warfarin > LMWH. Acute proximal or severe distal DVT: 3 months of DOAC. IVC filter only if anticoagulation is contraindicated. Treat superficial vein thrombosis if high-risk (prior VTE, surgery, cancer, within 3 cm of the saphenofemoral junction) {Lancet 2017:e105} |
| | **Cancer** | Apixaban {NEJM 2020;382:1599}, edoxaban {NEJM 2018;378:615}, rivaroxaban {JCO 2018;36:2017} or LMWH > warfarin {NEJM 2003;349:146}. **GI/GU cancers with luminal lesions:** apixaban or LMWH (edoxaban and rivaroxaban bleed more). Outpatient prophylaxis with apixaban or rivaroxaban if Khorana ≥2 {NEJM 2019;380:711; NEJM 2019;380:720} |
| | **Obesity** | Avoid DOACs if BMI ≥45 or weight ≥150 kg; use warfarin {Circulation 2024;149}. If a DOAC is used, check peak and trough levels {JTH 2021;19:1874} |
| | **Recurrence on treatment** | Not on LMWH → switch to LMWH. On LMWH → increase the dose by ¼–⅓ and check anti-Xa |
| **AF** | Non-valvular | DOAC > warfarin (fewer strokes, deaths and bleeds) |
| | Moderate–severe mitral stenosis | Warfarin, INR 2–3, regardless of CHA₂DS₂-VASc |
| | **After PCI** | Dual (P2Y12 + anticoagulant) beats triple: less bleeding, similar events {Annals 2020;172:474; EHJ 2019;40:3757}. Anticoagulant + clopidogrel for 6–12 months, then anticoagulant alone {NEJM 2019;381:1103}. Doses: rivaroxaban 15 mg daily {NEJM 2016;375:2423}; dabigatran 150 mg BID {NEJM 2017;377:1513}; apixaban 5 mg BID {NEJM 2019;380:1509}; edoxaban 60 mg daily {Lancet 2019;394:1335}; warfarin also works {Lancet 2013;381:1107}. Ticagrelor in hospital or for very high thrombotic risk. If triple therapy is chosen, stop aspirin at 1–4 weeks |
| **Valves** | Mechanical | **Warfarin only** (no DOACs). Aortic: INR 2.5 (3 if AF, VTE, etc.). Mitral/tricuspid: INR 3. On-X aortic: add aspirin |
| | Bioprosthetic | Surgical AVR: warfarin (INR 2.5) + aspirin for 3–6 months, then aspirin. TAVR: aspirin + clopidogrel 3–6 months, then aspirin. With AF/VTE: anticoagulant + clopidogrel, then anticoagulant |
| **APLS** | | Warfarin; beats rivaroxaban in high-risk APLS {Blood 2018;132:1365; AIM 2019;171:685} |
| **CAD** | ACS without PCI | Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BID + DAPT lowers CV mortality but raises major bleeding {NEJM 2012;366:9} |
| | Secondary prevention | Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BID + aspirin improves CV outcomes; more major bleeding, but not ICH or fatal bleeding {NEJM 2017;377:1319}. Consider if event risk is high and bleeding risk low |
| | After PCI | DAPT 12 months; 1–3 months of DAPT then clopidogrel is non-inferior {JAMA 2019;321:2414}. Use the DAPT score |

## Around procedures
{ACC: JACC 2017;69:871 · ASH: Blood Adv 2018;2:3257 · CHEST: Chest 2022;162:E207}

### DOACs: just hold them (no bridge)
| Drug | High bleeding risk, CrCl >50 | High risk, CrCl <50 | Low bleeding risk, CrCl >50 | Low risk, CrCl <50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dabigatran** | ≥48 h (4 doses) | ≥96 h (8 doses) | ≥24 h (2 doses) | ≥48 h (4 doses) |
| **Rivaroxaban** | ≥48 h (2 doses) | ≥48 h (2 doses) | ≥24 h (1 dose) | ≥24 h (1 dose) |
| **Apixaban** | ≥48 h (4 doses) | ≥48 h (4 doses) | ≥24 h (2 doses) | ≥24 h (2 doses) |
| **Edoxaban** | ≥48 h (2 doses) | ≥48 h (2 doses) | ≥24 h (1 dose) | ≥24 h (1 dose) |

- Bleeding risk = the procedure's risk + HAS-BLED.
- **Restart** 24 h after a low-risk procedure, 48–72 h after a high-risk one. If NPO or more procedures are coming, use UFH/LMWH in between.

### Warfarin: bridge only if thrombotic risk is high
| Thrombotic risk | AF | VTE | Mechanical valve |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High → bridge** (unless major bleed or ICH <3 months) | CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥7 (CHADS₂ 5–6); stroke/TIA or embolism <3 months; rheumatic valve disease | VTE <3 months; severe thrombophilia (protein C/S or antithrombin deficiency, APLS, multiple abnormalities) | Any mechanical mitral; caged-ball or tilting-disc aortic; any mechanical valve with stroke/TIA <6 months |
| **Moderate → individualize** (weigh procedural bleeding risk) | CHA₂DS₂-VASc 5–6 (CHADS₂ 3–4); stroke/TIA or embolism >3 months | VTE 3–12 months; recurrent VTE; active cancer; non-severe thrombophilia (heterozygous factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation) | Bileaflet aortic with ≥1 risk factor: AF, prior stroke/TIA, HTN, diabetes, heart failure, age >75 |
| **Low → don't bridge** | CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≤4 (CHADS₂ 0–2), no prior stroke/TIA | VTE >1 year, no other risk factors | Bileaflet aortic without AF or risk factors |

- **BRIDGE trial:** bridging warfarin in AF added bleeding without preventing embolism (excluded mechanical valves, recent stroke/TIA, recent major bleeding, CrCl <30, platelets <100K). {NEJM 2015;373:823}

:::key How to bridge warfarin
1. Stop warfarin **5 days** before (if INR is therapeutic); start LMWH (preferred if kidney function allows) or UFH once INR <2
2. Stop UFH **4–6 h** and LMWH **12 or 24 h** before surgery (depending on dosing interval)
3. Restart UFH/LMWH **24 h** after a low-risk procedure or **48–72 h** after a high-risk one; stop once INR >2
4. Restart warfarin **within 24 h** if no bleeding (it takes 24–72 h to act, so it doesn't add early bleeding)
:::
