# Acid–Base Disorders
> Six steps: pH, primary process, anion gap (always, corrected for albumin), compensation, delta gap, osmolar gap. Then treat the cause; bicarbonate is a bridge, not a fix. {NEJM 1998;338:26; NEJM 2014;371:1434}

## Six steps
1. **Acidemia** (pH <7.35) or **alkalemia** (pH >7.45)?
2. **Primary process:** metabolic (HCO3 low in acidosis, high in alkalosis) or respiratory (PCO2 high in acidosis, low in alkalosis)?
3. **Anion gap**, whatever the pH or HCO3: **AG = Na − (Cl + HCO3)**. Correct for albumin: **AG + 2.5 × (4 − albumin)**.
4. **Compensation as expected?** (table below). Respiratory compensation takes minutes to hours; renal takes 3–5 days.
5. **High-AG acidosis → delta gap** = (corrected AG − 12) + HCO3. **<18:** also a non-gap acidosis. **18–30:** pure gap acidosis. **>30:** also a metabolic alkalosis.
6. **Osmolar gap** = measured osm − [2(Na + K) + BUN/2.8 + glucose/18 + EtOH/4.6]. **>10** → toxic alcohols (also pseudohyponatremia).

| Disorder | Expected compensation {Kidney360 2022;3(8)} |
|---|---|
| **Metabolic acidosis** (2–24 h) | **Winter's:** PCO2 = 1.5 × HCO3 + 8 ± 2 |
| **Metabolic alkalosis** (starts in 30 min, complete by 24 h) | PCO2 = 0.7 × HCO3 + 20 ± 5 |
| **Respiratory acidosis** | Acute: HCO3 up 0.1 × ΔPCO2 (1 per 10). Chronic: up 0.35 × ΔPCO2 |
| **Respiratory alkalosis** | Acute: HCO3 down 0.2 × ΔPCO2. Chronic: down 0.41 × ΔPCO2 |

- **Negative anion gap:** very high Na or other cations (Ca, Mg, lithium), hyperlipidemia (Cl assay interference), paraprotein (myeloma), bromide or iodide.
- **VBG vs ABG:** VBG pH ~0.04 lower, HCO3 ~2 lower (the BMP is more accurate), PCO2 3–8 higher. A VBG PCO2 <45 rules out hypercapnia (100% sensitive) but doesn't measure its degree; unreliable in severe shock. When in doubt, get an ABG. {AJEM 2012;30:896}
- **Why it matters:** pH <7.2 → vasodilation, weaker contractility, poor pressor response, arrhythmias, hyperkalemia, insulin resistance, AMS. pH >7.6 → vasoconstriction (less coronary and cerebral flow), SVT/VT, low K/Ca/Mg/phosphate, AMS, seizures, hypoventilation.

## Causes
::: High anion gap acidosis | GOLD MARKS
**G**lycols (ethylene, propylene) · **O**xoproline (chronic acetaminophen) · **L**actate · **D**-lactate (short gut) · **M**ethanol · **A**spirin · **R**enal failure · **K**etoacidosis (alcohol, DKA, starvation) · **S**odium thiosulfate
- Possible ingestion → check the **osmolar gap**. <10: salicylates, acetaminophen (5-oxoproline). >10: send a volatile panel.
:::
::: Non-gap acidosis | RAGES, then urine anion gap
**R**TA · **A**mmonia, acetazolamide, hyperalimentation · **G**I losses, GU conduit · **E**ndocrine · **S**aline
- **Urine AG** = Na + K − Cl (estimates urine NH4+; less useful if urine pH >6.5, urine Na <25 or high aldosterone)
  - **Negative** ("neg-GUT-ive"): kidneys are excreting NH4+ normally → GI loss, saline, fistula, ureteral diversion, acetazolamide
  - **Positive:** NH4+ excretion impaired → kidney failure or RTA (distal if K low, hypoaldosteronism if K high)
:::
::: Metabolic alkalosis | Check urine Cl
Generated by gaining HCO3 or losing acid; maintained by low volume (angiotensin II, aldosterone), low Cl or low K.
- **Urine Cl <20 (saline-responsive):** vomiting or NG suction, post-hypercapnia, CF, chloridorrhea
- **Urine Cl >40 (saline-resistant):**
  - With HTN: high aldosterone (Conn, renal artery stenosis), Cushing, Liddle, licorice, low K
  - With low BP: Bartter, Gitelman, loop or thiazide diuretics (urine K >30 = diuretics; urine K <20 = laxatives)
:::
::: Respiratory | See Hypoxemia & Hypercapnia
- **Acidosis:** low drive (central), weak bellows (pleural/neuromuscular, low tidal volume), dead space (lung/airway)
- **Alkalosis:** anxiety, stroke, pain; pregnancy, fever, cirrhosis, sepsis, hyperthyroidism; aspirin, steroids, β-agonists, progesterone
:::

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Methanol** | ↑ | ↑ | Formic acid | AMS, blurry vision, dilated pupils, papilledema |
| **Ethylene glycol** | ↑ | ↑ | Oxalic acid | AMS, cranial nerve palsies, flank pain, hematuria, tetany (low Ca), oxalate crystals, AKI |
| **Propylene glycol** | ↑ | ↑ | Lactic acid | AKI, liver injury |
| **Diethylene glycol** | ↑ | ↑ | Diglycolic acid | AKI, nausea, hepatitis, pancreatitis, neuropathy, later AMS, high lactate |

| **Ethanol** | Normal/↑ | ↑ | Acetaldehyde | Keto/lactic acidosis ± alkalosis from vomiting |

## Treat the cause
### Metabolic acidosis
- **Bicarbonate:** BICAR-ICU (pH <7.2, IV HCO3 to pH >7.3) didn't change overall mortality but reduced dialysis, and **improved 28-day survival with AKI stage 2–3**. {Lancet 2018;392:31}
- **pH <7.1 or HCO3 <6:** if stable, an isotonic bicarbonate drip. VBG/ABG q1h with K, ionized Ca and volume checks. Boluses are controversial (CO2 load, hypernatremia, low Ca, volume, overshoot alkalosis).
- The patient must be able to raise ventilation to blow off the CO2.
- Non-gap and uremic acidosis respond to bicarbonate better than lactic and ketoacidosis.
- **Toxic alcohols:** call poison control; bicarbonate, fomepizole, or HD (vision change, AKI, high-AG acidosis, methanol >50 mg/dL, ethylene glycol >300 mg/dL).
- **Salicylates:** bicarbonate to urine pH >6.5 and level <40; HD if level >80 mg/dL, coma, AKI, volume overload or AMS; dextrose fluids if AMS. **Acetazolamide is contraindicated.**
- **Chronic (CKD):** oral NaHCO3 to HCO3 >22 (see *Chronic Kidney Disease*).

### Metabolic alkalosis
Fix the cause **and** whatever keeps the kidney holding HCO3 (volume, K, Cl).
- **Saline-responsive:** NS with KCl until urine pH >7. If alkalosis comes from diuresis in heart failure or cirrhosis: acetazolamide and/or a K-sparing diuretic (see *Advanced Diuresis*).
- **Saline-resistant (mineralocorticoid excess):** amiloride (faster than spironolactone); consider adrenalectomy for an adenoma.
- **pH >7.6 and still overloaded:** acetazolamide + loop diuretic, watching K closely.

### Respiratory
- **Acidosis:** treat the cause; adjust the ventilator. Bicarbonate rarely helps and can harm if the patient can't exhale the CO2 (100 mEq HCO3 → 2.2 L CO2 to exhale).
- **Alkalosis:** correct hypoxemia, pain, anxiety or fever; adjust the ventilator.

## Renal tubular acidosis {Int J Clin Pract 2011;65:350}
Consider with a hyperchloremic non-gap acidosis (or hyperkalemia for type IV) after ruling out GI losses and heavy NS use. In acidemia the kidney should excrete NH4Cl and drop urine pH below 5.3; RTAs can't. CKD of any cause also lowers NH4+ production.
| | Proximal (type II) | Distal (type I) | Type IV |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defect** | Low proximal HCO3 reabsorption | Low distal H+ secretion | Low distal K and acid secretion (hypoaldosteronism) |
| **Serum HCO3** | 12–20 | <10 | >17 |
| **Serum K** | Low/normal | Low/normal | **High** |
| **Urine pH in acidemia** | Variable; >5.5 after bicarbonate | >5.5 | <5.5 (but can't buffer with NH4+) |
| **Urine AG** | | Positive | Positive |
| **Next test** | Normal urine Ca/Cr; give bicarbonate and recheck urine pH | High urine Ca/Cr | Renin, aldosterone, cortisol |
| **Complications** | Rickets, osteomalacia | Calcium phosphate stones | Hyperkalemia |
| **Treatment** (HCO3 goal 22–24) | Hard: NaHCO3 10–20 mEq/kg/day or K citrate | NaHCO3 1–4 mEq/kg/day | Treat K (loop diuretic, low-K diet); fludrocortisone if hypoaldosteronism |

- **Proximal causes:** primary (rare); amyloid, myeloma, post-transplant, obstruction, heavy metals, low vitamin D, Wilson, PNH; drugs (acetazolamide, cisplatin, tenofovir, aminoglycosides, topiramate). Often **Fanconi**: glucosuria with glucose <180, low uric acid, aminoaciduria.
- **Distal causes:** genetic; autoimmune (RA, SLE, Sjögren), hypercalciuria, obstruction, sickle cell, myeloma, amyloid, cryoglobulinemia, tubulointerstitial disease, transplant rejection, cirrhosis, toluene; drugs (amphotericin B, lithium, ifosfamide, ibuprofen, codeine).
- **Type IV causes:** hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism (most common: diabetic nephropathy, chronic interstitial nephritis, NSAIDs, calcineurin inhibitors, HIV); low aldosterone (ACEi/ARB > heparin > adrenal insufficiency, critical illness); aldosterone resistance (K-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, pentamidine). High K blocks ammoniagenesis, so NH4+ excretion falls.
- **Work-up:** history (autoimmune disease, cancer, meds, stones), response to bicarbonate; VBG, BMP, UA pH, urine electrolytes; urine Ca/Cr to separate proximal from distal.
