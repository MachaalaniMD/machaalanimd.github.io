# Hypoxemia & Hypercapnia
> Respiratory failure means the lungs can't oxygenate (PaO2 <60), can't ventilate (PaCO2 >45), or both. For hypoxemia, the A–a gradient and the response to O2 point to the mechanism; for hypercapnia, ask "won't breathe" vs "can't breathe".

## Start here
- **Hypoxemic:** PaO2 <60. **Hypercapnic:** PaCO2 >45.
- **P/F ratio** (PaO2/FiO2) is a quick stand-in for the A–a gradient; P/F <300 → think ARDS (see *ARDS*).
- **Pulse oximetry can overestimate SpO2 in patients with darker skin,** delaying recognition of hypoxemia. Get an ABG when it matters. {JAMA 2022;182(7)}

## Hypoxemia: mechanisms
| Mechanism | A–a gradient | Fixes with O2? | Causes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypoventilation / low FiO2** | Normal | Yes | Altitude; any cause of hypercapnia |
| **V/Q mismatch** | High | Yes | **Focal** alveolar filling (pus, edema, blood, cancer, aspiration, alveolar proteinosis); airways (asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis); vessels (pulmonary HTN, PE) |
| **Shunt** (perfusion without ventilation) | High | **No** (refractory) | **Diffuse** alveolar filling incl. ARDS; collapse (pneumothorax, atelectasis, mucus plug); intracardiac/pulmonary shunt (PFO, ASD, VSD; AVMs e.g. hepatopulmonary syndrome) |
| **Impaired diffusion** (low DLCO) | High | Yes | ILD (tracks with CT severity), pulmonary HTN, advanced COPD. Worse with exertion |

- Hypoxic vasoconstriction diverts blood away from poorly ventilated lung and improves V/Q matching.
- With an intracardiac shunt, high PEEP can worsen hypoxemia (more West zone 1–2 lung raises RV afterload and right-to-left flow).

## Hypercapnia: mechanisms
::: Won't breathe | Low respiratory drive
Sedatives, obesity hypoventilation, brainstem stroke/tumor/infection, central sleep apnea, compensation for metabolic alkalosis, myxedema coma.
:::
::: Can't breathe | Low alveolar ventilation
- **More dead space** (ventilation without perfusion): anatomic ~150 mL; alveolar (West zone 1) is ~0 in health but rises as capillaries are destroyed or compressed:
  - Parenchyma: emphysema, ILD/fibrosis, heart failure, pneumonia, ARDS
  - Airways: asthma/COPD, CF, bronchiectasis, OSA, tumor, high PEEP
  - Vessels: massive PE
- **Restricted chest wall or pleura:** effusion, pleural fibrosis, obesity, kyphoscoliosis, abdominal distension, pneumothorax
- **Neuromuscular** {Neurol Clin 2012;30:161}: shows when the patient can't increase ventilation (early ALS often has *low* PaCO2 from hyperventilation). Trend single-breath count (awake) or NIF (intubated); consider EMG. Causes: neuropathy (C-spine/phrenic, GBS, ALS, polio), NMJ (myasthenia, botulism), myopathy (polymyositis/dermatomyositis, low phosphate), critical illness.
:::
::: Makes too much CO2 | High VCO2
High work of breathing, fever, seizures, sepsis, steroids, overfeeding, thyrotoxicosis.
:::

## Expected compensation
| Disorder | Acute | Chronic |
|---|---|---|
| **Respiratory acidosis** (PaCO2 up 10) | HCO3 up 1 | HCO3 up 3–4 |
| **Respiratory alkalosis** (PaCO2 down 10) | HCO3 down 2 | HCO3 down 5 |

See *Acid–Base Disorders*.
